\begin{abstract}
Standard Dirac quantum walks take their mass coin as an independent parameter.
Let $\psi,\phi\in\C^2$ represent two future-null rank-one momenta, set
$P_{\rm tot}=\psi\psi^\dagger+\phi\phi^\dagger$, and let
$z=\psi\wedge\phi$.  Cauchy--Binet gives
\[
  \det P_{\rm tot}=|z|^2.
\]
Relative to a fixed chiral grading $\Gamma$, the oriented area determines the
normalized odd Hermitian mass operator
\[
  B_z=\begin{pmatrix}0&z\\ \overline z&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  B_z^2=\det(P_{\rm tot})\id,\qquad
  (k\Gamma+B_z)^2=(k^2+\det P_{\rm tot})\id.
\]
We prove that this assignment is the unique positively homogeneous,
phase-equivariant map with the displayed grading and normalization.  The total
momentum determines the orbit radius $|z|=\sqrt{\det P_{\rm tot}}$; an oriented
null factorization selects a representative $B_z$ of that orbit.  The positive
rest energy is $|z|$, the spectral band gap at rest is $2|z|$, and both vanish
exactly when the spinors are collinear.  The construction derives the operator
and its zero locus, but does not select an absolute mass scale.

Exponentiating $B_z$ gives an exactly unitary null-step walk.  Opposite turns
carry $z$ and $\bar z$, fixed-momentum symbols converge to Dirac flow uniformly
on bounded momentum and mass sets at rate $O(1/n)$, and local chiral changes of
basis induce exact endpoint links.  A common phase is removable, but transported
relative phase is not.  For two equal-modulus sites coupled by an edge phase
$w$, the exact free Hamiltonian has squared energies
\[
  E_\pm^2=|z_L|^2+t^2
  \pm t\left|z_R-(\overline w)^2z_L\right|.
\]
The mismatch modulus is invariant under independent local chiral-frame changes,
and the lower level vanishes exactly at
$t=|z_L|$ and $z_R=-(\overline w)^2z_L$.  Thus null-spinor orientation supplies
a free one-particle observable after transport.  An independently assigned
complex mass field can reproduce the same local phase physics; the Pl\"ucker
contribution is its finite equivariant derivation, its determinant relation,
and its collinearity zero locus from one primitive spinor input.

The finite statements and their negative controls are formalized in Lean~4.
We also prove that the tested successive-axis $3+1$ extension retains a
classified family of zero- and $\pi$-quasienergy aliases that no
momentum-independent onsite coin removes.  An alias-free exactly local $3+1$
regulator, a complete interacting field dynamics, and absolute scale selection
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: one invariant, one operator, one walk}

The sum of lightlike momenta need not be lightlike.  In the spinor
representation, each future null momentum is a rank-one positive matrix
$\psi\psi^\dagger$.  A collection of such momenta remains on the null boundary
exactly while its spinors occupy one complex direction.  Once two directions
open, their exterior area is nonzero and the sum moves into the timelike
interior.

This familiar geometry contains a sharper operator statement.  The total
momentum determines the radius $|z|=\sqrt{\det P_{\rm tot}}$ of an odd-Hermitian
mass orbit.  An oriented null factorization retains the phase of
$z=\psi\wedge\phi$ and therefore selects a representative $B_z$ of that orbit.
After a chiral grading and one normalization are fixed, equivariance makes the
assignment unique.  The operator squares to the invariant momentum determinant,
anticommutes with the velocity grading, and exponentiates to an exact unitary
turn gate.  Transport between local chiral frames then converts relative
orientation into a gauge-invariant spectral mismatch.

The null spinors in this statement are factors of a momentum representation,
not additional asymptotic particles.  The construction does not predict an
absolute mass scale; a common mass phase is a basis choice; and covariance of
the input map under $\mathrm{SL}(2,\C)$ does not make the finite lattice dynamics
exactly Lorentz invariant.  These boundaries are part of the claim, not
qualifications added after it.

Three claims organize the paper.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Geometry and naturality.}  $P_{\rm tot}$ determines the radius
  of the odd-Hermitian mass orbit; an oriented factorization selects its
  representative.  Positive homogeneity, phase covariance, the chiral grading,
  and one normalization uniquely select $z\mapsto B_z$.
  \item \textbf{Dynamics and observability.}  $B_z$ generates an exactly
  unitary walk, directed histories, and a uniform $O(1/n)$ continuum-symbol
  bound.  Local phase transport produces the gauge-invariant mismatch
  $|z_R-(\overline w)^2z_L|$, which splits an exact free spectrum.
  \item \textbf{Regulator boundary.}  The tested successive-axis $3+1$
  extension has completely classified zero- and $\pi$-quasienergy aliases;
  no momentum-independent onsite coin removes them.  This is a theorem about
  that architecture, not a universal no-go for local $3+1$ walks.
\end{enumerate}

The main logical chain is therefore
\[
 \text{null geometry}\longrightarrow B_z\longrightarrow
 \text{unitary histories}\longrightarrow\text{transport}\longrightarrow
 \text{relative-phase spectrum}.
\]
The extended regulator and Fock calculations are retained as boundary and
companion results, but they do not carry the central novelty claim.

The result sits between spinor-helicity kinematics and discrete relativistic
quantum dynamics \cite{ElvangHuang,ArkaniHamedMassive}.  Cauchy--Binet and the
Pauli map supply the classical foundation.  Feynman's checkerboard and modern
quantum walks show how a massive Dirac propagator can arise from null steps and
direction changes
\cite{FeynmanHibbs,JacobsonSchulman,BisioDarianoTosini,DarianoPathIntegral,MlodinowBrun}.
Our contribution is the exact finite map from the oriented spinor area to the
odd rest operator and then to a controlled unitary walk, together with a
machine-checked dependency boundary.

\subsection*{Nearest constructions and the exact novelty claim}

For constant $z$, the update in this paper is unitarily equivalent to the
standard $1+1$ split-step Dirac walk.  We do not claim otherwise.  What is new
is the input map and its naturality: the coin is generated by the complex
Pl\"ucker coordinate of null momentum factors, its modulus gives the rest
energy, its phase acts by internal conjugacy and survives in oriented turn
amplitudes, and the resulting dependency chain is machine checked.  Table
\ref{tab:comparison} positions that claim against the closest constructions.

The Pavia programme moreover derives the Weyl and Dirac automata from
informational first principles \cite{FreeQCA}; the derivation offered here
is geometric rather than axiomatic, and the two are answers to different
questions.

The kinematic layer itself has long roots, and we claim none of it.
Weyl--van der Waerden and massive spinor-helicity formalisms routinely
decompose a massive momentum into two null spinors, with
$\det K=\lambda_1\lambda_2=k^2$ appearing explicitly in the
eigen-decomposition of the momentum bispinor \cite{DittmaierWvdW}, and the
bold-spinor formalism of Arkani-Hamed, Huang, and Huang
\cite{ArkaniHamedMassive} makes the two-null-spinor description of massive
states the organizing principle of amplitude kinematics.  Those formalisms
use the decomposition as covariant bookkeeping for scattering amplitudes.
The present paper starts where that bookkeeping stops: the wedge coordinate
is promoted to the off-diagonal entry of a canonical odd Hermitian operator,
the operator is classified (Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}), exponentiated
into an exactly unitary walk, tracked through directed histories and a local
connection, and every finite statement in that chain is kernel-checked.  The
amplitude formalisms neither assert nor need such a dynamical chain; the
overlap is the classical $2\times2$ Cauchy--Binet layer, which we label
\Classical{} throughout.

\section{Null-spinor area and the future cone}

\subsection{From rank-one momenta to area}

Let $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_N\in\C^2$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum}
 M=\begin{bmatrix}\psi_1&\cdots&\psi_N\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 P=MM^\dagger=\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i\psi_i^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Each summand is positive semidefinite and has rank at most one.  Under the
mostly-minus Pauli map, it is a future null four-momentum.  The determinant of
$P$ is the Lorentzian square of the total momentum.

\begin{theorem}[Null-spinor area identity]\label{thm:area}
For the matrix in \eqref{eq:momentum},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:area}
 \det P
 =\norm{\bigwedge{}^2 M}_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\sum_{i<j}\left|\det\begin{bmatrix}\psi_i&\psi_j\end{bmatrix}\right|^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\det P\geq0$, and $\det P=0$ if and only if the occupied columns
span a subspace of dimension at most one.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\Classical{} \Kernel{}

\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Binet applied to $MM^\dagger$ expresses its determinant as the sum of
the squared moduli of all $2\times2$ minors of $M$.  Those minors are exactly
the coordinates of $\bigwedge^2M$.  Every summand is nonnegative, so the sum
vanishes exactly when every pairwise wedge vanishes, equivalently when
$\rank M\leq1$.
\end{proof}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \mu:=\norm{\bigwedge{}^2M}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt{\det P}.
\end{equation}
The quantity $\mu$ is the first-order rest energy; $\mu^2$ is the Lorentzian
quadratic invariant.  The exterior norm makes the geometric word ``area''
literal rather than metaphorical.

For readers who prefer spatial directions, write the null momenta as
$p_i=E_i(1,\mathbf n_i)$ with $E_i\geq0$ and $|\mathbf n_i|=1$.  With the same
mostly-minus normalization,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angular}
 \mu^2
 =2\sum_{i<j}E_iE_j\bigl(1-\mathbf n_i\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf n_j\bigr).
\end{equation}
Mass squared is therefore a positive sum of directional disagreements.  It is
zero only when every occupied direction agrees; no cancellation between
different pairs is available.

\subsection{Why the determinant is canonical}

The determinant is not selected merely because it gives the desired answer.
On the physical space of Hermitian $2\times2$ matrices it is the unique real
quadratic form, up to scale, that vanishes on the full rank-one positive
boundary.

\begin{proposition}[Null-boundary uniqueness]\label{prop:unique}
Let $q$ be a real quadratic form on $\operatorname{Herm}(2)$.  If
$q(\psi\psi^\dagger)=0$ for every $\psi\in\C^2$, then
$q(P)=c\det P$ for some $c\in\R$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\Classical{} \Kernel{}

\begin{proof}
Identify $P\in\operatorname{Herm}(2)$ with $(t,\mathbf x)\in\R^{1,3}$ so that
$\det P=t^2-|\mathbf x|^2$.  Write
$q(t,\mathbf x)=a t^2+2t\,\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf x+\mathbf x^T C\mathbf x$,
with $C$ symmetric.  On the future null cone, homogeneity reduces the
hypothesis to $q(1,\mathbf n)=0$ for every unit vector $\mathbf n$.  Comparing
$\mathbf n$ with $-\mathbf n$ gives $\mathbf b=0$.  Hence
$\mathbf n^TC\mathbf n=-a$ on the unit sphere, which forces $C=-aI$ by
polarization.  Thus $q=a(t^2-|\mathbf x|^2)=a\det P$.
\end{proof}

The Lean theorem uses the four real coordinates of a Hermitian matrix and nine
explicit rank-one spinor probes to eliminate every quadratic coefficient except
one multiple of $pq-r^2-s^2$.  The older real-symmetric slice and a form that
fails the full-boundary hypothesis remain as independent controls.

\begin{corollary}[First-order uniqueness]
Suppose $g$ is nonnegative and homogeneous of degree one on the future cone,
and $g(P)^2$ is a quadratic form vanishing on the rank-one boundary.  Then
$g(P)=c\sqrt{\det P}$ for some $c\geq0$.
\end{corollary}
\noindent\Classical{} \Human{}

This is the logical bridge from a canonical mass-squared invariant to a
canonical positive rest energy.  The normalization $c=1$ fixes units.

\section{The Pl\"ucker mass operator}

Two copies of $\C^2$ play different roles.  Let
$S\simeq\C^2$ be the null-spinor space and let
$C_{\rm ch}=C_+\oplus C_-$ be the two-channel chiral space of the walk.
The area lies in the one-dimensional line $\bigwedge^2S$, whereas the rest
operator acts on $C_{\rm ch}$.  Choosing an oriented volume form on $S$, the
grading $C_+\oplus C_-$, and a unit normalization identifies
$\bigwedge^2S$ with the weight-one off-diagonal line
$\operatorname{Hom}(C_-,C_+)$.  All uses of ``canonical'' below are relative
to exactly these choices.

For two spinors, retain the complex orientation data rather than immediately
discarding it into an absolute square:
\begin{equation}
 z:=\psi\wedge\phi=\psi_0\phi_1-\psi_1\phi_0,
 \qquad
 P_{\rm tot}:=\psi\psi^\dagger+\phi\phi^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:area} gives $|z|^2=\det P_{\rm tot}$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bz}
 B_z:=\begin{pmatrix}0&z\\ \overline z&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Gamma:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BzPauli}
 B_z=(\operatorname{Re}z)\sigma_x-(\operatorname{Im}z)\sigma_y.
\end{equation}
The map does not restrict the pointwise complex mass parameter: with the
standard basis $e_1,e_2$ of $S$, $\psi=e_1$ and $\phi=z e_2$ give
$\psi\wedge\phi=z$ for every $z\in\C$.  Its content is geometric provenance,
the determinant relation, and the exact zero locus, not a smaller set of
allowed complex masses.

\begin{theorem}[Pl\"ucker-derived rest operator]\label{thm:Bz}
The matrix $B_z$ is Hermitian and odd for the grading $\Gamma$.  More
precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bzidentities}
 B_z^\dagger=B_z,
 \qquad
 \Gamma B_z+B_z\Gamma=0,
 \qquad
 B_z^2=|z|^2\id=\det(P_{\rm tot})\id.
\end{equation}
For
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hz}
 H_z(k):=k\Gamma+B_z,
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hero}
 \boxed{H_z(k)^2=\left(k^2+\det P_{\rm tot}\right)\id}.
\end{equation}
The rest eigenvalues are $\pm|z|$, and $B_z=0$ exactly when the two nonzero
spinor directions are collinear.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\NewResult{} \Kernel{}

\begin{proof}
Hermiticity is immediate from the two off-diagonal entries.  Direct
multiplication gives
$B_z^2=\diag(z\overline z,\overline z z)=|z|^2\id$, while the diagonal signs
in $\Gamma$ give the anticommutator zero.  Expanding $H_z(k)^2$ leaves
$k^2\id+B_z^2$ because the mixed term vanishes.  Finally $B_z=0$ if and only
if $z=0$, and a two-spinor wedge vanishes exactly on projective collinearity.
Every step of this proof is represented by a general Lean declaration, not by
evaluation of a fixed example.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:Bz} is stronger than assigning the scalar $\det P_{\rm tot}$ to an
otherwise independent Dirac mass slot.  The odd operator is constructed from
the oriented Pl\"ucker coordinate itself; its square derives the quadratic
invariant.  The distinction between powers is now fixed:

\subsection{Phase covariance, naturality, and the mass orbit}

If $u\in\C$ has $|u|=1$, let $W_u=\diag(u,1)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phasecov}
 W_uB_zW_u^\dagger=B_{uz},
 \qquad
 W_uW_u^\dagger=\id.
\end{equation}
Both identities are kernel-checked.  Thus the phase of $z$ rotates the internal
basis but cannot change the rest spectrum.

Section~\ref{sec:phasespectrum} uses the determinant-one chiral frame

\begin{theorem}[Classification of odd Hermitian rest operators]
\label{thm:classification}
Let $B\in M_2(\C)$ be Hermitian and satisfy
$\Gamma B+B\Gamma=0$.  Then there is a unique $w\in\C$ such that
\begin{equation}
 B=B_w=\begin{pmatrix}0&w\\\overline w&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B^2=|w|^2\id.
\end{equation}
Consequently the odd Hermitian square roots of $\mu^2\id$ form one phase
circle $w=\mu u$, $|u|=1$, and for $\mu>0$ they are all related by the
conjugacies in \eqref{eq:phasecov}.  Specifying the oriented Pl\"ucker
coordinate $z$ selects one member of that orbit.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\NewResult{} \Kernel{} for the normal form and square;
\Human{} for the phase-orbit corollary.

\begin{proof}
Oddness forces both diagonal entries of $B$ to vanish, and Hermiticity forces
the lower-left entry to be the complex conjugate of the upper-right entry
$w=B_{01}$.  The square is then $|w|^2\id$.  If it equals $\mu^2\id$, then
$|w|=\mu$; for $\mu>0$, write $w=\mu u$ with $|u|=1$ and apply
\eqref{eq:phasecov}.
\end{proof}

The classification theorem describes the target space.  The following
naturality statement is what makes the assignment itself unique.

\begin{theorem}[Equivariant uniqueness of the normalized rest map]
\label{thm:equivariantuniqueness}

$F:\C\to M_2(\C)$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]

  \item $F(0)=0$ and $F(rz)=rF(z)$ for every $r\geq0$;
  \item $F(uz)=W_uF(z)W_u^\dagger$ whenever $|u|=1$;
  \item $F(1)=\sigma_x$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $F(z)=B_z$ for every $z\in\C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

$r=|z|>0$ and $|u|=1$.  Positive homogeneity, equivariance, and normalization

\[

\]
\end{proof}

\section{Gauge-covariant phase transport and a free spectral observable}
\label{sec:phasespectrum}

At one point, every $B_z$ with the same $|z|$ belongs to the same chiral
conjugacy orbit.  Relative orientation becomes observable only after local
representatives are compared through transport.  Consider two chiral spaces
$C_L\oplus C_R\simeq\C^4$.  For $|w|=1$ and $t\geq0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H2blocks}
 D(w)=\diag(w,\overline w),\qquad
 T(t,w)=t\,\Gamma D(w),\qquad
 H_2(z_L,z_R,w,t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}B_{z_L}&T(t,w)\\T(t,w)^\dagger&B_{z_R}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
In the ordered basis $(L_+,L_-,R_+,R_-)$ this is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H2matrix}
H_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&z_L&tw&0\\
\overline z_L&0&0&-t\overline w\\
t\overline w&0&0&z_R\\
0&-tw&\overline z_R&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

Independent local chiral-frame changes $b_L,b_R\in U(1)$ act by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localphaseaction}
 z_L\mapsto b_L^2z_L,\qquad
 z_R\mapsto b_R^2z_R,\qquad
 w\mapsto b_Lw\overline b_R.
\end{equation}
Direct conjugation by
$V_{b_L,b_R}=\diag(b_L,\overline b_L,b_R,\overline b_R)$ gives the transformed
Hamiltonian.  The transported mismatch
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Delta}
 \Delta:=z_R-(\overline w)^2z_L
\end{equation}
therefore transforms as $\Delta\mapsto b_R^2\Delta$, so $|\Delta|$ is gauge
invariant.  The square on $w$ reflects the half-phase carried by each chiral
component.

\begin{theorem}[Transported-phase spectral polynomial]
\label{thm:phasespectrum}
Assume $t\geq0$, $|z_L|=|z_R|=\mu$, and $|w|=1$.  Then $H_2$ is Hermitian and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phasepolynomial}
 \boxed{\left(H_2^2-(\mu^2+t^2)\id\right)^2
 =t^2|\Delta|^2\id.}
\end{equation}
Consequently the squared energies are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phaseenergies}
 E_\pm^2=\mu^2+t^2\pm t|\Delta|.
\end{equation}
When $t|\Delta|>0$, each level has total multiplicity two.  The lower level
vanishes exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phasezeromode}
 t=\mu,\qquad z_R=-(\overline w)^2z_L.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

the eigenvalue polynomial, trace balance, and the zero-mode equivalence are

\subsection{Exponentiating the derived rest term}

For $\mu=|z|>0$, set $\widehat B_z=B_z/\mu$.  Since
$\widehat B_z^2=\id$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expB}
 e^{-\ii aB_z}
 =\cos(a\mu)\id-\ii\sin(a\mu)\widehat B_z.
\end{equation}
At $\mu=0$ the expression is understood by continuity and equals $\id$.
Combining the turn with the conditional null translation gives the momentum
symbol
\begin{equation}\label{eq:walk}
 U_a(k;z):=e^{-\ii ak\Gamma}e^{-\ii aB_z}.
\end{equation}
Each factor is unitary.  The two components translate in opposite null
directions; only the odd factor changes direction.

The finite formula in \eqref{eq:expB} is itself closed under composition:
writing it as $C_z(a)$, one has
\begin{equation}
 C_z(a)C_z(a)^\dagger=\id,
 \qquad
 C_z(a)C_z(b)=C_z(a+b).
\end{equation}
The explicit rest eigenvectors, right-unitary decomposition theorem, and this
one-parameter group law are all \Kernel{}.

By phase covariance, $U_a(k;z)$ is unitarily conjugate to the real-mass walk
with parameter $\mu$.  Its determinant is one and its trace is
$2\cos(ka)\cos(\mu a)$.  Therefore an eigenphase $e^{-\ii\omega a}$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dispersion}
 \boxed{\cos(\omega a)=\cos(ka)\cos(\mu a)}.
\end{equation}
At $k=0$ the exact eigenphases are $e^{\mp\ii a\mu}$, so the positive
principal-branch quasienergy is $\mu$.  Quasienergy is periodic modulo
$2\pi/a$; no global unbranched equality is asserted.  The real-mass spectral
statement and oriented mass-coin group law are \Kernel{}; the displayed
spectral conjugacy is an immediate corollary of the kernel-checked phase

\subsection{The exact corner ratio}

Equation~\eqref{eq:expB} gives two kinds of matrix element.  A component that
keeps its direction contributes $\cos(a\mu)$.  A direction change contributes
$-\ii\sin(a\mu)$ times $z/\mu$ or $\overline z/\mu$, according to its
orientation.  Hence an $n$-step history $h$ with $c(h)$ turns has amplitude
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathweight}
 A_a(h)
 =e^{\ii\Phi_k(h)}
   \cos(a\mu)^{n-c(h)}
   \bigl(-\ii\sin(a\mu)\bigr)^{c(h)}
   \Xi_z(h),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_k(h)$ is the translation phase and $\Xi_z(h)$ is a product of
unit-modulus orientation factors.  Whenever $\cos(a\mu)\neq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tanweight}
 A_a(h)
 =e^{\ii\Phi_k(h)}\cos(a\mu)^n
   \bigl(-\ii\tan(a\mu)\bigr)^{c(h)}\Xi_z(h).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Unitary checkerboard correspondence]\label{prop:tan}
After removal of the common factor $\cos(a\mu)^n$, the exactly unitary walk is
a corner-weighted checkerboard sum with finite-spacing corner parameter
$\tan(a\mu)$ and the orientation phase inherited from $z$.  Under the opposite
checkerboard sign convention, the parameter changes sign.  In either
convention,
\begin{equation}
 \tan(a\mu)=a\mu+O(a^3),
\end{equation}
but $\tan(a\mu)$ and $a\mu$ are not identical at finite spacing.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\Classical{} for the coin parametrization; \NewResult{} \Kernel{} for
the exact two-weight recursion scaling and the separate transfer-matrix
identification; \NewResult{} \Kernel{} for the directed complex orientation
bookkeeping in the same path sum.

\begin{proof}
Insert the two matrix elements from \eqref{eq:expB} at every time slice.  A
history with $c(h)$ off-diagonal selections and $n-c(h)$ diagonal selections
has exactly the product in \eqref{eq:pathweight}.  Factoring one
$\cos(a\mu)$ from every slice gives \eqref{eq:tanweight}.  The Taylor statement
is the standard expansion of the tangent.
\end{proof}

and specialization to $s=\cos(a\mu)$ and

\section{Full lattice spectrum, symmetry, and doubling audit}
\label{sec:spectrum}

Quantum-walk claims cannot be judged only at small momentum.  Introduce the
dimensionless variables
\[
 q=ka\in[-\pi,\pi],
 \qquad
 \theta=\mu a,
 \qquad
 \Omega=\omega a.
\]
The Brillouin zone is periodic in $q$, and quasienergy is periodic modulo
$2\pi$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete two-band audit]\label{thm:bands}
The two quasienergy bands are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bands}
 \Omega_\pm(q)
 =\pm\arccos\!\bigl(\cos q\cos\theta\bigr)
 \pmod{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
They are degenerate only when $q\in\pi\mathbb Z$ and
$\theta\in\pi\mathbb Z$.  On the physical principal branch
$0<|\theta|<\pi/2$, the walk is gapped throughout the Brillouin zone: the
positive rest energy at $q=0$ is $|\theta|/a=\mu$, the interband zero-energy
gap is $2\mu$, and the circular quasienergy gap at the zone edge is also
$2|\theta|$.  At $\theta=0$ there are two massless cones:
\begin{equation}
 (q,\Omega)=(0,0)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 (q,\Omega)=(\pi,\pi).
\end{equation}
For a differentiable band away from a degeneracy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:groupvelocity}
 v_g=\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k}
 =\frac{\sin q\cos\theta}{\sin\Omega},
 \qquad |v_g|\leq1,
\end{equation}
with strict inequality for a massive angle whenever the denominator is
nonzero.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\Realization{} \Kernel{} for the trace-derived dispersion, velocity
deficit inequality, exact massless set $\{0,\pi\}$, and uniform massive
discriminant bound; \Human{} for the implicit-differentiation identity in
\eqref{eq:groupvelocity} and for packaging these facts into the displayed
arccos branches.

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:bands} is the principal solution of
\eqref{eq:dispersion}.  A two-band degeneracy requires
$\sin\Omega=0$, equivalently
$|\cos q\cos\theta|=1$.  Each factor has absolute value at most one, so equality
forces $q,\theta\in\pi\mathbb Z$.  At $q=0$ one has
$\Omega_+=|\theta|$ on the stated branch; at $q=\pi$ one has
$\Omega_+=\pi-|\theta|$, whose circular distance to the negative band across
the quasienergy boundary is $2|\theta|$.  Implicit differentiation gives
\eqref{eq:groupvelocity}.  Finally,
\[
 \sin^2\Omega-\sin^2q\cos^2\theta
 =1-\cos^2\theta
 =\sin^2\theta\geq0,
\]
which proves the speed bound and its strict massive form.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Doubling and pseudo-doubling verdict.}
The high-momentum $(\pi,\pi)$ cone is a Floquet zone-edge partner of the
low-energy cone.  It is not a second zero-quasienergy root, but it is a second
relativistic band-touching point in the full lattice spectrum.  Following the
terminology of Gupta and Short \cite{GuptaShortDoubling}, it is naturally
classified as a pseudo-doubler: a high-quasienergy mode with a Dirac-like local
dispersion.  The elementary walk therefore does \emph{not} establish a
no-doubling or no-pseudo-doubling theorem.  The bounded
continuum estimate in Section~\ref{sec:continuum} isolates fixed physical
momenta as $a\to0$, sending $q\simeq\pi$ to cutoff momentum; that decoupling is
not the same as removing the finite-lattice partner.

\paragraph{Chiral time frame.}
Let
\[
 U_{\mathrm s}(q,\theta)
 =e^{-\ii\theta\sigma_x/2}
  e^{-\ii q\sigma_z}
  e^{-\ii\theta\sigma_x/2}.
\]
It is unitarily equivalent to $U(q,\theta)$ and obeys the exact chiral relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chiral}
 \sigma_yU_{\mathrm s}(q,\theta)\sigma_y
 =U_{\mathrm s}(q,\theta)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Thus the reciprocal pairing of quasienergies is structural, not an artifact of
the trace calculation.  Equation~\eqref{eq:chiral} is the symmetry statement
used here; a finer antiunitary or Floquet topological classification is left to
a separate analysis \cite{AsbothObuse}.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
If $z$ is perturbed to $z+\delta z$, then
\[
 \bigl||z+\delta z|-|z|\bigr|\leq|\delta z|.
\]
Therefore every perturbation with $|\delta z|<|z|$ remains off the collinear
gap-closing locus and retains a rest energy at least
$|z|-|\delta z|$.  The phase may rotate the internal basis, but by
\eqref{eq:phasecov} it cannot change a constant-coefficient spectrum.

\section{Uniform many-step Dirac control}
\label{sec:continuum}

The finite walk should approximate the continuous Dirac flow over a fixed
macroscopic time, not merely at one infinitesimal step.  For the real

\begin{equation}
 U(q,r)=e^{-\ii q\sigma_z}e^{-\ii r\sigma_x},
 \qquad
 H(k,\mu)=k\sigma_z+\mu\sigma_x,
 \qquad
 V_t(k,\mu)=e^{-\ii tH(k,\mu)}.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform bounded-momentum product limit]\label{thm:rate}
For $K,M\geq0$, define
\begin{align}
 C_{\mathrm{box}}(K,M)
   &=2K^2+2M^2+KM^2+K^2M+KM,\\
 D_{\mathrm{box}}(K,M)
   &=4C_{\mathrm{box}}(K,M)
     +4(K+M)^2e^{K+M}.
\end{align}
If $|k|\leq K$, $|\mu|\leq M$, $n>0$, and $|t/n|\leq1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 \norm{
   U\!\left(k\frac{t}{n},\mu\frac{t}{n}\right)^n
   -V_t(k,\mu)}
 \leq D_{\mathrm{box}}(K,M)\frac{t^2}{n}.
\end{equation}
The bound is uniform on the displayed parameter box, and its right-hand side
tends to zero as $n\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof architecture]
The one-step walk differs from $\id-\ii(t/n)H$ by an explicit quadratic
remainder.  The exact exponential differs from the same first-order matrix by
another quadratic remainder, giving
$\norm{U-V_{t/n}}\leq D(k,\mu)t^2/n^2$.  Both matrices are unitary, so the power
telescope has no norm-growth factor:
\[
 \norm{U^n-V_{t/n}^n}\leq n\norm{U-V_{t/n}}.
\]
The exact short-time flows compose to $V_t$, producing the $1/n$ rate.  Finally
$D(k,\mu)\leq D_{\mathrm{box}}(K,M)$ on the parameter box.  Lean checks the
entrywise remainder estimates, operator-norm conversion, unitarity, telescope,
flow composition, box inequality, and limit.
\end{proof}

For $z=\mu u$ with $|u|=1$, the diagonal unitary $W_u$ commutes with $\Gamma$
and conjugates $B_\mu$ to $B_z$.  Unitary invariance of the operator norm
therefore transfers \eqref{eq:rate} to the oriented Pl\"ucker walk.  This
one-line conjugacy corollary is \Human{} pending a combined declaration.

The theorem proves uniform convergence of the finite momentum symbol in
$1+1$ dimensions.  It is not yet an infinite-volume inverse-Fourier theorem,
a position-space PDE convergence theorem, or an interacting limit.  Those
quantifiers are separate problems, not implications hidden in the word
``continuum.''

\section{Finite position kernels and a controlled $3+1$ extension}
\label{sec:threeplusone}

The uniform estimates already imply a finite position-space statement.  Let
$\mathcal K$ be a finite momentum set, let $\chi_x(k)$ be phases with
$|\chi_x(k)|\leq1$, and define the synthesized matrix kernel
\[
 \mathcal S_x[A]:=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\chi_x(k)A(k).
\]
Applying the triangle inequality to Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} gives the
kernel-checked bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitefourier}
 \norm{\mathcal S_x[U_n]-\mathcal S_x[V_t]}
 \leq |\mathcal K|D_{\mathrm{box}}(K,M)\frac{t^2}{n}.
\end{equation}
This controls every finite periodic Fourier kernel.  The cardinality factor is
the generic price of applying the triangle inequality pointwise.  It disappears
at the level of normalized $L^2$ wave packets.  For Mathlib's unnormalized
vector-valued transform $\mathcal F$ on $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, we prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plancherel}
 \sum_k
   \norm{(\mathcal F f)(k)}^2
   =N\sum_x
   \norm{f(x)}^2,
 \qquad
 \sum_x
   \norm{\mathcal F^{-1}g(x)}^2
   =\frac1N\sum_k
   \norm{g(k)}^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently, if a modewise error obeys
$\norm{\delta(k)}\leq\varepsilon\norm{c(k)}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l2wavepacket}
 \sum_x\norm{\mathcal F^{-1}\delta(x)}^2
 \leq \frac{\varepsilon^2}{N}
       \sum_k\norm{c(k)}^2.
\end{equation}
Both identities and the implication are \Classical{} \Kernel{}; a one-mode
packet is included as an exact normalization control.  We also prove the
operator-valued corollary: a uniform bound
$\norm{A(k)-B(k)}\leq\varepsilon$ implies
\eqref{eq:l2wavepacket} for
  $\delta(k)=(A(k)-B(k))c(k)$.  Applying that corollary to the local walk
  uses the exact plane-wave block stated below.  Iterating the transform in
  the three coordinates gives, for $N\geq1$, the kernel-checked product-torus identity
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:plancherel3}
    \sum_{x\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^3}
      \norm{\mathcal F_3^{-1}g(x)}^2
    =\frac{1}{N^3}
      \sum_{k\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^3}\norm{g(k)}^2.
  \end{equation}
  Hence, again for $N\geq1$, a uniform modewise operator error $\varepsilon$ gives the exact
  wave-packet coefficient $\varepsilon^2/N^3$, with no cardinality loss.  The
  momentum-sign bridge is also kernel-checked: for the positive-exponential
  convention the finite character block equals the analytic block at
  $k_j\varepsilon=-2\pi n_j/L$, including an exact quarter-zone $-\ii$ sign
  control.

  There is also a countable successor to the finite Plancherel statement.  If
  $e_n(k)$ is a countable family of mode errors such that
  $e_n(k)\to0$ for every $k$ and
  $\norm{e_n(k)}^2\leq b(k)$ for one summable envelope $b$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:countablel2}
    \sum_k \norm{e_n(k)}^2\longrightarrow0.
  \end{equation}
  This is a direct vector-valued Tannery specialization, proved in Lean with a
  nonzero one-mode control.  It isolates the remaining physical obligation:
  construct the walk-specific square-summable envelope and identify the
  limiting multiplier with the Dirac PDE propagator under a continuum Fourier
  isometry.

  The corresponding measure-theoretic multiplier step is also closed.  If
  $f\in L^2$ and pointwise errors satisfy
  $\norm{e_n(k)}\leq\varepsilon_n\norm{f(k)}$ almost everywhere, where
  $0\leq\varepsilon_n$ and $\varepsilon_n\to0$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:continuousl2multiplier}
    \norm{e_n}_{L^2}\longrightarrow0.
  \end{equation}
  The proof is a kernel-checked `eLpNorm` monotonicity-and-squeeze argument.
  The walk-specific compact-support application is also proved.  For any
  fixed $t\in\mathbb R$, suppose $f\in L^2$ and, almost everywhere on the support of $f$,
  $|k_x|,|k_y|,|k_z|\leq K$ and $|z|\leq M$, with $K,M\geq0$.  Applying the
  complex split symbol for $n+1$ steps gives an error multiplier $E_n$ satisfying
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:walkcompactl2}
    \norm{E_nf}_{L^2}\longrightarrow0,
    \qquad
    \norm{E_n(k)f(k)}
      \leq\frac{D_{K,M}t^2}{n+1}\norm{f(k)}.
  \end{equation}
  The displayed pointwise envelope holds for all sufficiently large $n$; the
  finite prefix is irrelevant to the sequence limit.
  This walk-specific composition is \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  What remains is
  the lattice/continuum Fourier-isometry and identification of the limiting
  multiplier with the position-space Dirac PDE, not compact-support multiplier
  convergence.

  We now also close the changing-scale multiplier gate in $3+1$ dimensions.
  At refinement level $N$, set
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:scaledgrid}
    h_N=\frac{1}{N+1},\qquad K_N=(N+1)^2,\qquad
    W_N=3(N+1)+M+1,qquad n_N=W_N^4.
  \end{equation}
  The momentum grid spacing tends to zero, while its represented center radius
  is exactly $h_NK_N=N+1$ and therefore exhausts momentum space.  For every
  integer mode $k_N$ in the complete cutoff box and every fixed mass
  $|m|\leq M$, the live four-component split step obeys the uniform bound
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:scaledlivebound}
    \left\|U_{h_Nk_N}\!\left(\frac{t}{n_N}\right)^{n_N}
      -e^{-\ii tH(h_Nk_N,m)}\right\|
    \leq \frac{2t^2}{W_N^2}
      \exp\!\left(\frac{|t|}{W_N^3}\right)
    \longrightarrow0.
  \end{equation}
  This changing-spacing, exhausting-box theorem is \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.
  Its boundary fixture takes the positive $x$-face mode, whose represented
  physical momentum is exactly $N+1$; the result is therefore not a disguised
  fixed-compact-momentum limit.

  A concrete cell-sampling dense core is also available.  Let $f$ be a
  complex Lipschitz momentum field with constant $L$, and let a finite family
  of the half-open cells of side $h_N$ cover its support.  Sampling $f$ at each
  cell center gives the exact global estimate
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:cellsamplingrate}
    \int_{\mathbb R^3}|I_Nf(k)-f(k)|^2\,dk
      \leq \#S_N\,h_N^3\left(\frac{Lh_N}{2}\right)^2.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently, if the represented cell volume $\#S_Nh_N^3$ is uniformly
  bounded and $h_N\to0$, the left side tends to zero.  Cell radius $h_N/2$ in
  the product sup norm, exact union volume, support reduction, and the limit
  are all \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  This is strong $L^2(\mathbb R^3)$ convergence
  for a compact-support Lipschitz dense core.  The regularity bridge to
  arbitrary data is also now kernel-checked: every complex $L^2(\mathbb R^3)$
  field admits a compactly supported smooth approximant with arbitrarily small
  $L^2$ error, and that same approximant has one global Lipschitz constant.
  Compact support is load-bearing: the smooth noncompact quadratic
  $x\mapsto x_0^2$ has no global Lipschitz constant.

  There is a sharp representative-level obstruction to extending this center
  sampler to arbitrary $L^2$.  A function equal to one at a single cell center
  and zero elsewhere is zero almost everywhere, but center sampling reads the
  value one; sampling the AE-equal zero function reads zero.  Thus the center
  sampler is not AE-invariant and cannot define a bounded operation on $L^2$
  equivalence classes.  This no-go is \NoGo{} \Kernel{}, not merely an
  analytic warning.  Normalized cell averaging supplies the correct first
  repair: it is AE-invariant, sends the one-point spike to zero, and returns
  exactly one on the constant-one function for every positive cell size.

  The representative-safe repair now closes the changing-lattice flow theorem
  for arbitrary square-integrable data.  The finite cell-average projection is
  an exact $L^2$ contraction; compact support is eventually covered by the
  expanding schedule; only the active cells, not the full growing box, require
  a volume bound.  The capstone then separates the total error into three
  terms: live evolution versus the exact flow at each cell center, exact flow
  at the center versus exact flow at the running momentum, and exact evolution
  of the cell-projection tail.  Each term tends strongly to zero.  Thus, for
  every fixed $z\neq0$, fixed bound $M\geq|z|$, fixed time $t$, and fixed
  componentwise-$L^2$ four-spinor $F$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:changinglatticepositionl2}
    \left\|\mathcal F^{-1}\!\left(
      \text{live}_{N,z,t}P_NF-e^{-\ii tH_z}F\right)\right\|_{L^2}
      \longrightarrow0.
  \end{equation}
  Here the displayed live term abbreviates the cellwise reconstruction used in
  the formal statement, and $\mathcal F^{-1}$ is Mathlib's vector-valued
  inverse Fourier isometry.  The representative identity, all three norm
  limits, their triangle composition, and the position-space norm transfer are
  \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  This is a changing-lattice, full-$L^2$ convergence
  theorem for fixed mass, data, and time; it is not uniform over all masses or
  data, and it says nothing about interactions or the extra high-frequency
  sectors audited below.

  The exact comparison flow also has its expected generator on compact
  momentum support: the strong derivative at $t=0$ is pointwise
  $-\ii H_z(q)F(q)$.  Independently, the affine Hermitian symbol $H_z(q)$ now
  defines a densely defined maximal multiplication operator on momentum-space
  $L^2$.  Explicit bounded inverses for $H_z\pm\ii$ have operator norm at most
  one, map into the maximal graph domain, and make both imaginary shifts
  surjective.  The resulting unbounded Hamiltonian is therefore self-adjoint,
  equal to its adjoint, and closed; these statements are \NewResult{}
  \Kernel{}.

  The exact Fourier-conjugated maximal operator is now defined on the pulled-back
  graph domain.  Unitary conjugation proves it self-adjoint and closed, identifies
  its graph exactly, and preserves both graph-norm terms.  On Schwartz functions,
  the derivative/momentum bridge is also kernel checked with Mathlib's actual
  $2\pi$, sign, and $\ii$ convention.  What remains open is stronger: prove that
  this abstract maximal conjugate agrees with the classical differential Dirac
  expression on a graph core, and identify its full domain with a vector-valued
  Sobolev $H^1$ space.  Mathlib currently lacks the required vector-valued
  weak-derivative/weighted-Fourier characterization.  Convergence of derivatives
  and interacting dynamics remain later gates as well.

The same architecture extends to four internal components.  The
successive-axis factorization itself is the standard construction used by
Mlodinow and Brun \cite{MlodinowBrun}; what is new here is the
Pl\"ucker-derived complex coin, the exact finite-torus implementation with a
two-sided inverse and strict causal cone, and the kernel-checked all-zone
audit that follows.  Let Hermitian
matrices $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\beta\in M_4(\C)$ satisfy
\[
 \alpha_i^2=\beta^2=\id,
 \qquad \{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=0\ (i\neq j),
 \qquad \{\alpha_i,\beta\}=0,
\]
and set
\[
 H_4(\mathbf k,m)=\sum_{j=1}^3k_j\alpha_j+m\beta.
\]
The explicit matrices used in the artifact obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H4square}
 H_4(\mathbf k,m)^2=(|\mathbf k|^2+m^2)\id.
\end{equation}
For a substep $\varepsilon$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3plus1step}
 U^{(4)}_\varepsilon(\mathbf k,m)
 =e^{-\ii\varepsilon k_x\alpha_1}
  e^{-\ii\varepsilon k_y\alpha_2}
  e^{-\ii\varepsilon k_z\alpha_3}
  e^{-\ii\varepsilon m\beta}.
\end{equation}
The order in \eqref{eq:3plus1step} is not cosmetic: the spatial generators do
not commute, and the artifact contains that noncommutation as a negative
control against a false second-order claim.

\begin{theorem}[Finite local $3+1$ walk and compact rate]
\label{thm:3plus1rate}
On a finite periodic three-dimensional position register, there is an explicit
successive-axis update built from one-site conditional shifts and the mass coin
in \eqref{eq:3plus1step}.  It preserves the finite inner product exactly, has
an explicit two-sided inverse, and reads only sites reached by one conditional
source shift along each axis in one cycle.  Its product plane-wave sectors are
invariant, and on each sector the update is
exactly the ordered axis-$0$/axis-$1$/axis-$2$/mass matrix block built from
finite roots of unity.  For the positive-exponential plane-wave convention,
that block is \eqref{eq:3plus1step} when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:latticemomentum}
 k_j\varepsilon=-\frac{2\pi n_j}{L}.
\end{equation}
If
$|k_x|,|k_y|,|k_z|\leq K$, $|m|\leq M$, $n>0$, and $|t/n|\leq1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3plus1ratebound}
 \norm{\left(U^{(4)}_{t/n}(\mathbf k,m)\right)^n
       -e^{-\ii tH_4(\mathbf k,m)}}
 \leq 16(3K+M)^2e^{3K+M}\frac{t^2}{n}.
\end{equation}
Every finite Fourier synthesis obeys the same bound multiplied by its number
of modes, while the normalized product-torus $L^2$ wave-packet error obeys
the squared bound divided by $N^3$.  Moreover, let $B_4(z)$ be the
phase-retaining rest operator and let $U^{(4)}_\varepsilon(\mathbf k,z)$ be the
chiral conjugate of the displayed step at $m=|z|$.  Its exact comparison flow
is generated by
\[
 H_4(\mathbf k,z)=\sum_{j=1}^3k_j\alpha_j+B_4(z),
 \qquad
 H_4(\mathbf k,z)^2=(|\mathbf k|^2+|z|^2)\id,
\]
and whenever $|z|\leq M$ it satisfies the same bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complex3plus1rate}
 \norm{\left(U^{(4)}_{t/n}(\mathbf k,z)\right)^n
       -e^{-\ii tH_4(\mathbf k,z)}}
 \leq 16(3K+M)^2e^{3K+M}\frac{t^2}{n}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\noindent\Realization{} \Kernel{} for exact local norm preservation, Clifford
square, finite-character plane-wave block, compact rate, finite synthesis
bound, and the trigonometric/sign conversion
\eqref{eq:latticemomentum} to the analytic symbol; \NewResult{} \Kernel{} for
the complex-phase transfer \eqref{eq:complex3plus1rate} and the iterated
three-torus normalization \eqref{eq:plancherel3}.

\begin{proof}[Proof architecture]
Each $\alpha_j$ has eigenvalues $\pm1$.  An explicit unitary eigenbasis turns
the corresponding diagonal one-site conditional shift into the physical
Clifford factor; composing those shifts with the mass coin proves exact norm
preservation.  Direct substitution into every product plane wave extracts one
unit-modulus character per conditional shift and proves the exact ordered
finite block without an asymptotic argument.  The closed form
$e^{-\ii q\alpha_j}=\cos q\,\id-\ii\sin q\,\alpha_j$ identifies the ordered
analytic matrix symbol after the sign convention in
\eqref{eq:latticemomentum}.  A four-factor quadratic remainder estimate, exact
unitarity, and the unitary power telescope then give
\eqref{eq:3plus1ratebound}.  Applying the finite synthesis theorem gives its
position-kernel corollary.
\end{proof}

For $z\neq0$, the phase transfer is exact.  If $W_z$ is the chiral unitary at
the polar angle of $z$, then
\[
 W_zH_4(\mathbf k,|z|)W_z^\dagger=H_4(\mathbf k,z),
 \qquad
 W_ze^{-\ii tH_4(\mathbf k,|z|)}W_z^\dagger
   =e^{-\ii tH_4(\mathbf k,z)}.
\]
The operator norm cannot increase under this conjugation, so the real-mass
estimate transfers without changing its constant.

\subsection*{The $3+1$ high-symmetry verdict}

The continuum tangent is exactly isotropic:
$H_4(\mathbf k,0)^2=|\mathbf k|^2\id$.  The finite cubic walk is not globally
doubler-free.  At the eight corners
$(q_x,q_y,q_z)\in\{0,\pi\}^3$, its massless symbol is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cornerparity}
 U^{(4)}(q_x,q_y,q_z,0)=(-1)^{r}\id,
 \qquad
 r=\#\{j:q_j=\pi\}.
\end{equation}
Thus $(\pi,\pi,0)$, $(\pi,0,\pi)$, and $(0,\pi,\pi)$ are distinct momenta
 with exactly the same zero-quasienergy symbol as the origin.  Equation
 \eqref{eq:cornerparity}, all three distinctness witnesses, and radial isotropy
 of the continuum square are \Realization{} \Kernel{}.  This is an actual
 finite-regulator obstruction, not a caveat inferred from a plot.

The massive walk has a stronger interior obstruction.  At body-center momentum
$\mathbf q_B=(\pi/2,\pi/2,\pi/2)$, let
$u_\theta=\cos\theta+\ii\sin\theta$ and
\[
 v_+(\theta)=(-u_\theta,0,1,0)^T,
 \qquad
 v_-(\theta)=(u_\theta,0,1,0)^T.
\]
For every real mass angle $\theta$, the exact finite step obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bodycentermodes}
 U^{(4)}(\mathbf q_B,\theta)v_+(\theta)=v_+(\theta),
 \qquad
 U^{(4)}(\mathbf q_B,\theta)v_-(\theta)=-v_-(\theta),
 \qquad v_\pm(\theta)\neq0.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the body-center step is a non-scalar involution for all
$\theta$.  It therefore retains exact quasienergies $0$ and $\pi$ even after
the mass coin is turned on: the present cubic regulator admits no uniform
Floquet gap (a body-center crossing at $0$ and $\pi$ persists for every
mass angle).
The closed body-center matrix, involution identity, and both explicit
eigenmodes are \NoGo{} \Kernel{}.

The interior crossings moreover carry an exact charge bookkeeping.  At
each of the eight nodes $q_j\in\{\pi/2,3\pi/2\}$ the massive step has
two-dimensional $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces simultaneously, and the
Schur-reduced crossing Jacobian of every node and gap is one of exactly
two diagonal matrices, $\pm\tfrac45\,\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,-1)$, with
determinant $\pm(\cos\theta)^3$ at the $3$-$4$-$5$ angle --- every node
is a nondegenerate cone.  The resulting local charges (the Jacobian
determinant signs) are distributed by the parity of the node's
$3\pi/2$-coordinates, are opposite at the two quasienergies of each
node (the Floquet pairing), and sum to zero at each quasienergy
separately.  This is now machine-checked from the walk symbol itself.
The base census arithmetic --- the two Jacobian determinants, the
parity-indexed assignment, and its sum-zero --- is kernel-checked in
\texttt{SplitStepChargeBalance}; and the derivation of the Jacobians
\emph{from the Bloch symbol} is kernel-checked for all eight nodes in
\texttt{SplitStepSchurJetAllNodes}: the exact node collapse
$U_n=(-1)^{\operatorname{par}(n)}U_0$ (from $\cos\tfrac{3\pi}{2}=0$,
$\sin\tfrac{3\pi}{2}=-1$), the two-dimensional $\pm1$ kernels at every
node, the Schur-reduced $3\times3$ Jacobian, and the resulting per-node
Floquet opposition and sum-zero.  The derivation is anchored to the
landed census by a compiler-enforced bridge
(\texttt{CensusDerivationBridge}): the walk-derived assignment is proved
equal, at every node and gap, to the \texttt{SplitStepChargeBalance}
census imported from that module, so nothing rests on an unchecked copy.
The one modelling choice made explicit is that the nondegenerate-cone
reading uses the normal-matrix structure of each node.  The doublers of this walk are
not merely present: they are exactly the charge partners the sum rule
demands --- derived from the symbol --- and the two gaps compensate node
by node.

The obstruction is also visible in an exact all-zone criterion.  Write
$x=\cos q_x$, $y=\cos q_y$, $w=\cos q_z$, and $c=\cos\theta$, and define
\begin{align}
 S={}&4c^2x^2y^2w^2-2c^2x^2y^2-2c^2x^2w^2+c^2x^2
      -2c^2y^2w^2+c^2y^2+c^2w^2 \notag\\
   &{}-2x^2y^2w^2+x^2y^2+x^2w^2+y^2w^2,\\
 P_0&=S-2cxyw,
 \qquad
 P_\pi=S+2cxyw.
\end{align}
For the live ordered successive-axis symbol, the finite determinant expansion
is now exact at every real momentum and mass angle:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fullblochdet}
 \det\!\left(U^{(4)}-\id\right)=4P_0,
 \qquad
 \det\!\left(U^{(4)}+\id\right)=4P_\pi.
\end{equation}
Thus $P_0=0$ and $P_\pi=0$ are respectively necessary and sufficient for
zero- and $\pi$-quasienergy modes.  At body center $x=y=w=0$, both polynomials
vanish for every $\theta$, recovering \eqref{eq:bodycentermodes}.  The two
determinant identities, their explicit $4\times4$ expansion, and the
definitional bridge to the walk used in the continuum estimate are
\NoGo{} \Kernel{}.

The zero sets are now classified on the principal massive branch.  If
$0<|c|<1$ and $|x|,|y|,|w|\leq1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fullblochzeroset}
 P_0=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=y=w=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_\pi=0.
\end{equation}
The proof uses the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
 P_0=(cx-yw)^2+
 \bigl(y^2+w^2-2y^2w^2\bigr)
 \bigl(c^2+x^2(1-2c^2)\bigr),
\end{equation}
whose second factor is strictly positive and whose first prefactor is
nonnegative on this branch.  Equality then forces all three momentum cosines
to vanish; the $P_\pi$ statement follows by $c\mapsto-c$.  Thus the simultaneous
body-center cosine locus is the complete zero- and $\pi$-quasienergy crossing
set for every principal massive angle, not merely a sampled obstruction.
The hypotheses are load-bearing: at $c=0$, additional zeros occur, including
$(x,y,w)=(1,0,0)$.  The classification and this boundary control are
\NoGo{} \Kernel{}.  They classify the failure of the present regulator; they
do not make it alias-free.

\paragraph{A degree-one stationary-amplitude no-go.}
The most economical proposed repair is to add a stay-put channel to each
nearest-neighbor factor.  The exact ansatz is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationarylaurent}
 F(q)=e^{\ii q}A+B+e^{-\ii q}C.
\end{equation}
Let $M$ be any Hermitian involution.  If $F(q)$ is unitary for every real $q$,
$F(0)=\id$, and $F'(0)=-\ii M$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationarynogo}
 B=0.
\end{equation}
The proof evaluates the exact unitarity residual at $q=\pm\pi/2$; adding the
two resulting matrix identities forces $B^\dagger B=0$.  Specializing
$M=\alpha_j$ rules out a nonzero stationary amplitude in a degree-one factor
with the full Dirac tangent, even before zone-edge separation is imposed.
All three specializations to the live $3+1$ generators are separately
kernel-checked.  This is \NoGo{} \Kernel{}.  A nonvacuity control shows that a partial-channel
Hermitian tangent with a stationary kernel does admit an exactly unitary
degree-one witness with $B\neq0$ and a non-scalar zone edge.  That witness does
not retain the full Dirac tangent.  Thus stationary amplitudes remain viable
only after a genuine change of architecture, such as a larger internal cell,
longer Laurent range, multistep factorization, or a non-involutory tangent.

The last route is not hypothetical.  Gupta and Short
\cite{GuptaShortDoubling} construct doubler- and pseudo-doubler-free
nearest-neighbor one-step unitary walks, in $1+1$ and $3+1$ dimensions, whose
enabling resource is exactly a nonzero stay-put amplitude
$\gamma_0=\id-\gamma_+-\gamma_-$ built from tilted, non-orthogonal velocity
projectors (their Eqs.~(29)--(30) and~(37)).  Their step is exactly of the
degree-one Laurent form \eqref{eq:stationarylaurent}, unitary at every
momentum and normalized at the origin, with stationary block $\gamma_0$.
The no-go \eqref{eq:stationarynogo} therefore yields a corollary about
their family: its tangent generator, though automatically Hermitian for a
unitary origin-normalized step, cannot be an involution ($M^2
\neq\id$).
The stay-put freedom that removes the doublers is purchased
exactly by relinquishing the involutory unit-speed Dirac tangent, and our
partial-channel escape witness realizes the same mechanism in kernel-checked
form.  Their $3+1$ family moreover retains extraneous Weyl-like low-energy
solutions at isolated momenta $\pm\mathbf q(\theta)$, which the authors
suggest a more general higher-dimensional construction might remove (their
Appendix~F), so a strictly local, exactly unitary, fully alias-free $3+1$
walk with the complete involutory Dirac tangent remains open in both
programs.

The obstruction is stronger for a separable three-axis product.  Once
$B_x=B_y=B_z=0$, origin normalization forces each factor to obey
$F_j(\pi)=-\id$.  Therefore, for any momentum-independent onsite coin $Q$,
including the complex Pl\"ucker mass coin,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:genericcorneralias}
 U_Q(0,0,0)=U_Q(\pi,\pi,0)=U_Q(\pi,0,\pi)=U_Q(0,\pi,\pi).
\end{equation}
This all-coins alias theorem is \NoGo{} \Kernel{}.  It proves that changing
the onsite mass data cannot repair the cubic partners inside the
four-channel, range-one, single-factor-per-axis architecture.  Extra range,
coupled substeps, or enlarged/ancillary cells are not optional embellishments;
at least one is structurally required.

An attempted commutator--Wilson repair sharpens that requirement.  Consider
the exactly unitary one-axis gate
\begin{equation}
 W_r(q)=F_{\beta}(rq)F_{\gamma_5}(rq)
        F_{\beta}(-rq)F_{\gamma_5}(-rq),
 \qquad
 F_A(s)=\cos s\,\id-\ii\sin s\,A.
\end{equation}
Its linear term cancels, so at the Bloch-matrix level it appears to supply a
second-order Wilson-like correction without disturbing the Dirac tangent.
However, in the fixed one-cell ansatz strict finite Laurent range requires the
frequency $r$ to be an integer.  For every integer $r$ the exact zone-edge
identity is
\begin{equation}
 W_r(\pi)=\id,
\end{equation}
and all three even-corner aliases in \eqref{eq:genericcorneralias} remain
exactly equal to the origin.  Conversely, the noninteger trigonometric values
that lift selected corners are not $2\pi$-periodic finite Laurent polynomials
in $e^{\ii q}$ and therefore do not define the claimed strict local one-cell
QCA.  This family-scoped locality/de-aliasing incompatibility, including the
range-one control, is \NoGo{} \Kernel{}.  It does not exclude a larger cell or
a genuinely different finite product; it prevents a nonlocal Bloch control
from being mislabeled as that successor.

The matching cross-architecture algebraic invariant is now exact in one
Laurent variable.  The determinant of every invertible finite Laurent matrix
is a nonzero scalar times a uniquely exponented monomial, and that exponent is
additive under composition, vanishes on the identity, and equals $n$ on the
one-channel shift $T^n$.  This is the algebraic precursor of the
translation-invariant one-particle quantum-walk index in GNVW Proposition~5
\cite{GNVW}; identifying the two still requires a complex-unitary
Laurent/Fourier representation theorem and an explicit shift-sign convention.
It is not the positive-rational many-body GNVW QCA index, an entropy-flow
theorem, or a no-doubling result.

There is also a necessary correction to the charge strategy.  A complete
mass-admitting four-component Dirac crossing admits an explicit gapped
algebraic contraction to a constant.  In the standard class-A topological
reading this makes the complete crossing neutral, but that topological bridge
is not itself part of the kernel result.  If
$F_0(\mathbf n)=\sum_j n_j\alpha_j$ is the flattened tangent and $\beta$
anticommutes with it, then

\begin{equation}
 F_s(\mathbf n)=\cos(\pi s/2)F_0(\mathbf n)+\sin(\pi s/2)\beta
\end{equation}

satisfies $F_s(\mathbf n)^2=\id$ and ends at the constant matrix $\beta$.
The finite matrix identity, including explicit anticommuting involutions and a
missing-anticommutation control, is \Realization{} \Kernel{}.  The nonzero
local charges instead belong to the two Weyl sectors selected by
$\Xi=-\ii\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3$.  For the actual live cubic Clifford
tangent, an exact finite calculation constructs two orthogonal Weyl bases and
restricts $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)$ to the Pauli triple and its negative.
The resulting supplied-Jacobian charges are exactly $+1$ and $-1$, so their
four-component sum vanishes; a singular tangent has charge zero.  This is
\Realization{} \Kernel{} at the local tangent.  A sectorwise Brillouin-zone
argument still requires the additional global hypothesis
$[U(\mathbf q),\Xi]=0$ at every momentum; local chirality at the origin is
insufficient.  For the actual ordered Bloch step this gate is now exact:
\begin{equation}
 [U(\mathbf q,\theta),\Xi]=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sin\theta=0.
\end{equation}
The chirality is a Hermitian involution, and its positive-sector projector
commutes with the complete massless walk at every momentum.  The walk is
therefore globally Weyl-sector split, while every genuine mass angle
($\sin\theta\ne0$) mixes the sectors; an exact quarter-turn fixture gives a
nonzero commutator.  Here the mass weight is $\sin\theta$; a cosine variable
used in the separate determinant factorization is not that weight.  These
statements are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  They determine precisely when the
sectorwise no-go can be invoked, but it does not supply the missing global
charge-sum theorem.

The analytic and arithmetic core of that census is now machine-checked on the
$2\times2$ positive-Weyl block.  Lean extracts the exact Pauli vector of the
live ordered step, proves its complete real Fr\'echet derivative, and identifies
the derivative matrix with the one used in the finite census.  Its determinant
obeys
\begin{equation}
 \det J(\mathbf q)=u_0(\mathbf q)
   \bigl(\cos^2 q_y-\sin^2 q_y\bigr),
\end{equation}
evaluates to $\pm1$ at the sixteen designated principal-torus nodes, and the
actual live-Jacobian charges cancel separately on the displayed $U=+\id$ and
$U=-\id$ lists.  Every designated node consequently has a distinct
nondegenerate partner inside its finite list.  These are \NewResult{}
\Kernel{}.  A second exact theorem classifies the complete massless
$4\times4$ zero- and $\pi$-determinant sets in cosine coordinates: each is the
appropriate parity class of cube corners together with the body-center locus.
The branch-sensitive bridge is now exact.  For the actual positive
$2\times2$ Weyl block, $U=+\id$ holds if and only if either all three cosine
squares are one with cosine product $+1$, or all three cosines vanish with
sine product $-1$.  Likewise, $U=-\id$ holds exactly on the odd-parity cosine
corners or the body centers with sine product $+1$.  These two global
trigonometric loci are disjoint, and the actual Fr\'echet-Jacobian determinant
is nonzero at every point of either locus.  The rank-deficient quarter-axis
control lies on neither branch.  This is \NewResult{} \Kernel{} and upgrades
the displayed charge census from a selected list to an exhaustive positive-Weyl
branch classification.  It is still a Jacobian-sign doubling theorem, not a
formal Chern-number computation.  At a full $4\times4$ body center both
quasienergies remain present across the two Weyl blocks, which is why the
branch theorem could not be inferred from the full determinant set alone.

This distinction sharpens both the no-go and the escape.  Read's algebraic
$K$-theory calculation for complex Laurent automorphisms retains only the
constant unit and coordinate-delay classes, with no higher-dimensional strong
component in the change-of-rings image \cite{ReadLaurentKTheory}.  Together
with Floquet charge--bulk bookkeeping \cite{BesshoSatoFloquet}, this supplies a
source-supported route to a global-chirality doubling theorem, but the
finite-rank and zero/$\pi$ convention composition is not yet kernel-checked
here.  Conversely, the exact decomposition
$U_\perp=(U-\Xi U\Xi)/2$ proves that $U_\perp$ has zero constant and linear
coefficients when the normalized Dirac first jet commutes with $\Xi$.
Therefore the first construction class worth testing consists of exact
Laurent-unitary regulators with nonzero chirality mixing beginning beyond the
linear jet, not another globally chirality-preserving onsite coin.

That escape resource is now exact at coefficient level.  The quadratic
regulator $q(\mathbf k)R$, with $q(\mathbf k)=\sum_j k_j^2$ and an explicit
chirality-odd matrix $R$, vanishes at the Dirac point and has zero complete
Fr\'echet derivative there, yet equals a nonzero chirality-mixing matrix on a
unit-axis fixture.  This is \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  It proves that higher-order
mixing can preserve the desired first jet; it does not yet make the regulator
an exact finite-range unitary or exclude every unwanted crossing.

The compatible exact-unitary primitive is a four-factor group commutator of
circle phase steps.  Kernel-checked, it is unitary, becomes the identity when
either angle vanishes, gives $+\id$ for commuting involutions and $-\id$ for
anticommuting involutions at a quarter turn, and admits an explicit rational
mixed pair whose result is not any scalar matrix.  Its algebraic Lie
coefficient $GA-AG$ is chirality-odd whenever $A$ is chirality-even and $G$ is
chirality-odd; for the live $(\alpha_1,\beta)$ pair that coefficient is
nonzero and equals its full odd projection.  The complete first Fr\'echet
derivative of the commutator vanishes at the origin, while its mixed second
derivative is exactly $GA-AG$.  These are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  The same
exact algebra also proves a sharp negative result.  A common sign flip of either
cosine/sine pair leaves the commutator unchanged because the corresponding
central signs occur twice and cancel.  Hence every integer-frequency phase
commutator, including one with affine phase offsets, takes the same value at
all cubic $0/\pi$ corners as at the origin; every finite product of zero-offset
loops is exactly invisible there.  A single phase step supplies the negative
control and does see the sign flip.  Thus the obstruction belongs to the
doubled commutator architecture, not to phase gates individually.

A construction that survives that obstruction uses a genuinely
noncentral conditional shift.  With
$D(z)=\operatorname{diag}(z,1)$, an exact rational coin $C$, and
$K(z)=D(z)CD(z^{-1})C^{-1}$, define the reciprocal word
$S(z)=K(z)K(z^{-1})$.  Lean proves that $S$ is unitary on the circle,
$\det S=1$, $S(1)=\id$, and
$S(z)-\id=(z-1)^2Q(z)$.  Thus its constant and linear jets vanish exactly.
Its strict locality is also exact rather than inferred: $S$ has five Laurent
coefficients at powers $-2,-1,0,1,2$, both range-two coefficients are nonzero,
their zeroth moment is the identity, and their first moment vanishes.  After
multiplication by $z^2$, the word is an ordinary degree-four matrix polynomial.
At $z=-1$ the resulting rational matrix has both
$\det(S-\id)=2304/625$ and $\det(S+\id)=196/625$, so it removes both
zero- and $\pi$-crossings of the identity baseline at that corner.  This is
\NewResult{} \Kernel{} as a two-band finite-Laurent construction primitive,
not yet an alias-free $3+1$ walk.  A naive four-component embedding creates
additional generic roots in exact-arithmetic tests; any successful embedding
owes an exact all-torus root certificate.

The two-band primitive itself is now classified across the full rational
tangent-half-angle coin family.  Writing
$c=(1-r^2)/(1+r^2)$ and $s=2r/(1+r^2)$, the reciprocal word remains exactly
unitary with determinant one on the circle.  At $z=-1$, its zero-gap
determinant is nonzero exactly when $r\ne0,\pm1$, while its $\pi$-gap
determinant is nonzero exactly away from the roots of
$r^2-2r-1$ and $r^2+2r-1$.  The original $3$--$4$--$5$ coin is exactly the
$r=1/2$ member, and $r=0$ is the identity negative control.  These are
\NewResult{} \Kernel{} two-band corner statements.  They do not define the
four-component blocks or provide a global-torus embedding certificate.

There is a precise gate on one tempting certificate.  For a unitary
$4\times4$ step with $\det U=1$, the determinant $\det(U-\id)$ is exactly real:
the kernel-checked identity is
\[
 \overline{\det(U-\id)}=\det(U-\id).
\]
This makes a real intermediate-value argument available when two Weyl sectors
have paired determinant.  Conversely, real endpoint values alone do not make
$\det(U(q)-\id)$ real along a path.  The family-level reciprocal sign oracle
therefore remains conditional until the live $U_\pm(q)$ blocks, one consistent
register convention, paired determinant along an explicit origin-avoiding
path, and the endpoint formulas are composed in one theorem.  This condition is
a kernel-checked reusable reality gate, not a root-existence theorem.

At the two-band level there is a complementary exact locking theorem.  If
$U$ is unitary and $\det U=1$, then
\[
 \det(U-\id)=0\Longleftrightarrow U=\id,
 \qquad
 \det(U+\id)=0\Longleftrightarrow U=-\id.
\]
Thus determinant roots and whole-sector zero/$\pi$ crossings coincide in an
$SU(2)$ block; they are not merely statements that one eigenvalue happened to
hit the branch.  Exact determinant-minus-one controls show the conclusion fails
without unimodularity.  This is \NewResult{} \Kernel{} and supplies the legal
bridge between matrix-root and identity-preimage censuses used below.

A more serious escape attempt puts the reciprocal mixing inside a genuinely
chirality-coupled four-component coin, so that the regulator is noncentral at
the old corners and leaves the Dirac first jet only at quadratic order.  That
direct construction also fails.  On the exact slice $q_x=\pi$, $q_y=0$, its
zero- and $\pi$-crossing determinants reduce to squared reciprocal quartics.
With $x=z+z^{-1}=2\cos q_z$, their reduced factors have opposite signs on
$x\in[1,2]$ and $x\in[-1,0]$, respectively.  Hence the coupled word has at
least one nonzero-momentum crossing of each quasienergy on that slice.  Lean proves
both physical unit-circle existentials exactly: the reduced real quadratics
cross zero, the intermediate value theorem supplies roots, and $q_z$ is
reconstructed with $\arccos$, with $q_z\ne0$ in both cases.  This is
\NoGo{} \Kernel{} for the displayed direct construction, not a universal
reciprocal no-go.  The negative result is diagnostic: corner visibility and
higher-order chirality mixing can move the alias locus without reducing it.
An enlarged determinant-paired register or an explicitly minimally doubled
construction is the next construction class to test.

A second exact primitive shows that a stationary onsite amplitude need not
conflict with strict locality or unitarity.  For arbitrary orthogonal
projectors $P$ and $Q$, not assumed to commute, the controlled-phase product
has the range-one Laurent form
\[
 U(z)=z\Gamma_+ + \Gamma_0 + z^{-1}\Gamma_-,
 \qquad |z|=1,
\]
and is exactly unitary.  A rational noncommuting two-band witness has
$\Gamma_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}16/25&-12/25\\12/25&16/25\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\ne0$.  The necessary $z\ne0$ hypothesis and the correct same-family adjoint
laws were enforced during formalization after the originally proposed formulas
failed.  This is \NewResult{} \Kernel{} as a one-variable construction
mechanism inspired by the stationary-amplitude route of Gupta and Short.  It
does not yet supply a three-dimensional isotropic symbol, a Dirac tangent, or
an all-zone proof that only the intended Weyl cones remain.

One of those gaps has a constructive answer.  Three explicit rational
noncommuting projector pairs have first Laurent moments exactly
$(3/5)\sigma_x$, $(3/5)\sigma_y$, and $(3/5)\sigma_z$.  Every axis retains a
nonzero onsite amplitude, while their ordered three-axis product is exactly
unitary for every torus momentum and equals the identity at the origin.  Thus
strict locality, stationary amplitude, all-zone unitarity, and an isotropic
set of per-axis Weyl first moments are compatible in one \NewResult{} \Kernel{}
construction.  At the origin, the other two factors are identities, so the
product-level isotropic first jet follows by the ordinary product rule; that
combined derivative has not yet been packaged as a separate kernel theorem.
The claim stops there: the full zero- and $\pi$-crossing census of this new
symbol must determine whether it is minimally doubled or still more highly
aliased.

A global test is negative.  At the distinct torus phase
$(z_x,z_y,z_z)=(-1,1,-1)$, the complete ordered symbol is exactly the identity.
Both the matrix equality and distinctness from $(1,1,1)$ are \NoGo{}
\Kernel{}.  Thus the explicit stationary-amplitude construction is not
unique-cone.  This is not the hollow case of a constant identity symbol: at
$(z_x,z_y,z_z)=(i,1,1)$ its $(0,0)$ entry is exactly $16/25$, so the symbol is
kernel-proved nonconstant.  Exact-coefficient computation identifies two
additional off-corner identity roots.  One is the following kernel theorem:
\[
 (z_x,z_y,z_z)=
 \left(\frac{-9-40i}{41},\frac{-9+40i}{41},1\right),
 \qquad U(z_x,z_y,z_z)=I.
\]
Both nontrivial phases lie exactly on the unit circle and differ from $1$.
Conjugate pairing is load-bearing: replacing $z_y$ by $z_x$ makes the
$(0,1)$ matrix entry exactly $-1104/1681$, so the resulting symbol is not the
identity.  This off-corner crossing is \NoGo{} \Kernel{}.  A second, fully
off-axis crossing is exact as well.  In tangent coordinates its third
coordinate is the unique real root of
\[
  480t^5-575t^4-1026t^2+1440t-575=0,
  \qquad 149/100<t<3/2.
\]
Explicit rational quartic functions of $t$ reconstruct the other two tangent
coordinates.  Lean proves all three are nonzero, maps them to unit-circle
phases different from $1$, and proves that the actual imported live matrix is
exactly $U=I$.  The companion elimination sextic is strictly positive on all
of $\mathbb R$.  The corrected original-generator certificate is now exact in
Lean as well:
\[
 (1+t_z^2)^2 t_z p_5(t_z)p_6(t_z)
   =Q_xF_x+Q_yF_y+Q_zF_z.
\]
The three quotient polynomials contain 522 exact rational terms in total.
Kernel normalization proves this identity and, because $1+t_z^2>0$, proves
that simultaneous numerator zeros force the zero, quintic, or sextic branch.
The chart factor is mandatory: the corresponding bare polynomial is not in
the numerator ideal.  These are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  The formerly missing
live bridge is now exact: on the finite real tangent chart, Lean proves that
the actual imported matrix satisfies $U=I$ if and only if its scalar equation
and all three numerator equations hold.  On the full $t_z=0$ slice, those
three live numerator equations have exactly two real solutions, the origin
and $(-5/4,5/4,0)$; both reconstruct $U=I$, and the latter is precisely the
$9$--$40$--$41$ alias.  The interior census is now complete: exact
polynomial-ideal certificates force $t_x$ and $t_y$ to be the displayed
quartic reconstruction functions of $t_z$ on the unique real quintic branch.
Hence every finite-chart identity crossing is exactly the origin, the
$9$--$40$--$41$ point, or the unique fibre over that quintic root; the last
branch has a fully off-axis witness.  \NewResult{} \Kernel{}  An exact boundary
oracle finds only the already known $(-1,1,-1)$ identity crossing on the seven
phase-$-1$ chart strata, but that boundary classification remains a separate
formal gate.  The result is therefore an exhaustive finite-chart no-go with a
boundary-chart obligation, not a finished regulator.

A finite $\mathbb Z_2^3$ cover can fold the eight $\{0,\pi\}^3$ corner labels
to one reduced representative while retaining a unique three-bit flavour
label.  The cover is bijective, its deck action is free and transitive, and a
single diagonal $\mathbb Z_2$ bit is provably too small to label all corners.
This is \NewResult{} \Kernel{} as an exact census, but not a cure: it relabels
eight crossings and removes none.  For the present successive-axis symbol the
exact translation intertwiner reduces to a scalar parity sign.  Four flavour
labels preserve zero quasienergy and four exchange zero with pi; a one-axis
translation is explicitly nonidentity, while a pi/2 shift fails to be any
scalar deck action.  These statements are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.  This route
must therefore not be advertised as a unique-cone theorem or as a derivation
of observed particle generations: it is a faithful eightfold census.

The finite logical hinge is also kernel-checked: if a finite crossing set has
total integer Jacobian-sign charge zero and one crossing is nondegenerate, a
distinct nondegenerate crossing must exist.  The theorem makes the topological
input visible rather than hiding it: the zero-total premise still has to come
from an exact crossing census or the separately audited Laurent/Floquet
composition.

There is already a constructive Hamiltonian-level route around this no-go.
Using the same live Clifford generators, define
\begin{align}
 M_W(\mathbf q)&=m+r\sum_{j=1}^{3}(1-\cos q_j),\\
 H_W(\mathbf q)&=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sin q_j\,\alpha_j+M_W(\mathbf q)\beta.
\end{align}
The kernel-checked Wilson identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wilsonsquare}
 H_W(\mathbf q)^2=
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sin^2q_j+M_W(\mathbf q)^2\right)\id.
\end{equation}
For $m>0$ and $r\geq0$, the scalar coefficient is bounded below by $m^2$.
For $m=0$ and $r>0$, it vanishes if and only if
$\cos q_1=\cos q_2=\cos q_3=1$; hence all non-origin cubic corners are lifted.
For example, the $(\pi,0,0)$ energy squared is exactly $4r^2$.  This closes
the spectral design problem at the nearest-neighbor Hamiltonian level while
preserving the Dirac tangent.  It does \emph{not} yet close the stricter QCA
problem: $\exp(-\ii tH_W)$ is exactly unitary, but its finite-time exponential
is not asserted to be a finite Laurent polynomial or a strictly finite-range
one-step update.

\paragraph{Exact temporal blocking and nonclosure.}
Define the two-step blocking map by $\mathcal B(U)=U^2$.  The mass-only
Pl\"ucker subgroup closes exactly,
\[
 \mathcal B\bigl(C_z(\varepsilon)\bigr)=C_z(2\varepsilon),
\]
but the ordered spatial split family does not close by doubling its scalar
step.  At the exact $x$--$y$ quarter-turn witness,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rgcounterexample}
 \mathcal B\bigl(U_{xy}(\pi/2)\bigr)=-\id,
 \qquad U_{xy}(\pi)=\id.
\end{equation}
The smallest multiplicatively closed enlargement is the submonoid generated
by all bare split steps.  These statements are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}: temporal
blocking generates ordered word/coupling data rather than merely a
renormalized copy of the original scalar parameters.  Spatial decimation is a
separate, stronger RG problem.

\paragraph{A proved boundary on scale selection.}
For the finite positive Pl\"ucker action developed here, simultaneous scaling
of both primitive spinors by $t$ gives the exact law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quarticscalenogo}
 S(t\psi,t\phi;x)=t^4S(\psi,\phi;x).
\end{equation}
Whenever the unscaled action is positive, its radial equation
$4t^3S(\psi,\phi;x)=0$ is equivalent to $t=0$.  The orthogonal-spinor,
positive-quartet control has $S=1/2$, so the obstruction is nonvacuous.
The formal result is now more general.  For any positive finite action with
radial profile $S(tx)=t^pS(x)$ and natural degree $p>0$, the derivative at unit
scale is $pS(x)>0$, so the configuration cannot be stationary.  At $p=0$ the
radial direction is exactly flat and cannot select an isolated scale.  This is
\NoGo{} \Kernel{}: no positive fixed finite homogeneous action can
spontaneously select a nonzero scale.  A running/refining family, competing
homogeneity, constraint, ensemble scale, or other dimensionful input must be
displayed.
The exact positive control $V_c(t)=(t^2-c)^2$ has stationary points precisely
at $t=0$ or $t^2=c$; for supplied $c=1$, the nonzero points $t=\pm1$ are global
minima.  Selection is therefore possible, but its magnitude is exactly the
new input.  This control is also \Realization{} \Kernel{}.

\paragraph{Comparison with the tetrahedral walk.}
Nzongani et al.~\cite{NzonganiTetra} place four amplitudes on tetrahedral
facets, compose facet/strict/weakly local shifts with directional coins, and
insert the assigned coin $e^{-\ii\varepsilon m\gamma^0}$; their expansion
recovers the $3+1$ Dirac equation to first order.  Our register and finite
update are different: the present construction is a cubic successive-axis

unitary conjugacy preserves equality of symbols at distinct momenta, so every
 constant onsite conjugate of our walk inherits the three aliases above and
 the body-center $\{+1,-1\}$ spectrum.  Any equivalence with a tetrahedral update
 must therefore reproduce those obstructions
or use a non-onsite blocking, changed unit cell, or coarse-graining map.  A
full spectral audit of the tetrahedral symbol is needed before choosing among
those possibilities.

\paragraph{Small-step-sensitive $3+1$ rate.}
The live four-factor symbol now has a sharper fixed-time theorem.  With
$B_4=|k_x|+|k_y|+|k_z|+|m|$ and $n>0$,
\[
 \left\|U(t/n)^n-e^{-\ii tH}\right\|
 \leq {2B_4^2t^2\over n}\exp\!\left({|t|B_4\over n}\right).
\]
The proof keeps the small-step exponent through both the split-product
remainder and the unitary telescope; unlike the earlier compact-box envelope,
it does not replace this factor by $\exp(B_4)$.  The resulting growing-window
theorem takes $n=K^4$ and permits arbitrary momentum and mass sequences with
$B_4\leq K$; their modewise split-versus-exact error is bounded by
$2t^2K^{-2}\exp(|t|K^{-3})$ and tends to zero.  A sharp pure-momentum boundary
  fixture saturates $B_4=K$, so this is not a fixed-mode restatement.  The later
representative-safe cell projection and inverse-Fourier isometry close the
changing-lattice full-$L^2$ position-space convergence theorem for fixed mass,
data, and time.  Differential-domain and interacting limits remain open.

\subsection*{What mechanization changed}

The formal artifact is scientifically useful only where it improves the
mathematics.  In this development it forced ten corrections that are visible
in the paper.  First, it separated the first-order rest energy $\mu$ from the
quadratic invariant $\mu^2=\det P$ instead of allowing one symbol to drift
between operator orders.  Second, exact unitarity exposed the finite-spacing
corner ratio $\tan(a\mu)$, replacing the tempting but false finite identity
$a\mu=\tan(a\mu)$.  Third, proving an iterated product estimate separated a
one-step Taylor expansion from genuine uniform many-step control.  These are
not the only bookkeeping gains.  Fourth, the exact $3+1$ composition preserved
the noncommuting x/y/z/mass factor order instead of identifying reversed local
 updates.  Fifth, the $1+1$ full-zone audit separated the zero-quasienergy cone
 from its high-quasienergy Floquet partner.  Sixth, the $3+1$ high-symmetry audit
 disproved the tempting global reading of continuum isotropy: the tangent is
 isotropic, three nonzero corners exactly alias the origin, and at body center
 the massive step retains explicit $+1$ and $-1$ modes for every mass angle.
 Seventh, the exact Laurent residual ruled out the most economical proposed
 repair: a nonzero stationary amplitude cannot coexist with origin
 normalization, exact unitarity, and a full involutory tangent inside one
 degree-one nearest-neighbor factor.
 Eighth, the inhomogeneous phase calculation separated a global basis rotation
 from a genuine endpoint connection and proved that a global real lift cannot
 carry nonzero winding.  Ninth, exact temporal blocking showed that the
 mass-only group law closes while the noncommuting spatial split generates a
 larger ordered-word family.  Tenth, the radial derivative of the positive
 Pl\"ucker action proved that its homogeneity collapses rather than selects a
 nonzero scale.
 These are the formalization contributions; the number of declarations is not.

What can an assigned scalar mass mimic?  Everything that depends on $|z|$
alone: after setting $m=|z|$, the constant-coefficient spectrum, the
dispersion, the velocity bound, and the uniform many-step limit are
identical, and the paper says so without embarrassment.  What it cannot
mimic is now a theorem list rather than a narrative.  It has no input from
which the rest operator is derived rather than declared (the classification
theorem makes ``derived'' precise); it carries one real coin where the exact
history sum carries $z$ and $\bar z$ on opposite orientations; it produces
no endpoint connection, no integer link winding, and no patched transition
data from a varying phase unless that field is separately supplied; and a
scalar mass, being one real number, cannot reproduce the exact
$4/5$-versus-$1$ interference split in the supplied pair-kick sector.  More
importantly, it cannot reproduce the free two-site spectral split between
equal-modulus fields without importing exactly the complex datum and its
parallel transport that this paper derives.  A spatially common constant phase
remains removable, as it must; a transported relative phase is already a
free-spectrum observable.  The pair interaction is a second phase reader, not
the condition that makes the free reader exist.  Once the phase varies around
a cycle, the distinction further becomes patched link data with an integer
winding.

There is now also a free two-site spectral consequence.  For two equal-modulus
rest blocks coupled by the displayed unit edge phase, the exact $4\times4$
Hamiltonian obeys a scalar quadratic polynomial whose splitting is controlled
by the parallel-transported mismatch
$\Delta=z_R-(\overline w)^2z_L$.  Its lower squared-energy level vanishes
exactly at $t=|z_L|$ and
$z_R=-(\overline w)^2z_L$ (for $t\geq0$), while a common phase on both sites
is removed by an explicit unitary conjugacy.  The equal-modulus hypothesis is
load-bearing: the formal development contains a concrete unequal-modulus
counterexample.  This is a phase-sensitive finite free spectrum, not yet a
topological-protection or localization theorem.

The paper proves a finite free construction and one finite phase-sensitive
quartic extension.  It does not prove that every
massive particle is composite in the ordinary particle-physics sense.  The
``constituents'' here are null momentum factors of a finite spinor description,
not additional asymptotic particles.  Nor does the theorem compute an absolute
mass scale: rescaling the input spinors rescales $z$ and hence $\mu$.  The
quartic-action no-go sharpens this from an omission to a proved boundary for
the natural positive homogeneous action.

There is nevertheless a rigorous finite bridge from a chosen positive
Hamiltonian to the usual spectral signatures of mass.  For a finite Hermitian
$H\geq0$ and Euclidean spacing $a\geq0$, the genuine matrix exponential
$T=e^{-aH}$ is Hermitian, strictly positive, and contractive.  If a nonzero
observable vector is an eigenmode with energy $E>0$, then its transfer orbit is
exactly $e^{-aEn}v$, the energy is reconstructed as
$-a^{-1}\log(e^{-aE})$, and its scalar transfer resolvent has a unique simple
denominator zero with positive coefficient $\lVert v\rVert^2$.  Every finite
orbit kernel is Gram positive.  These statements are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.
They show that decay, reconstructed energy, and positive visible pole weight
are not independent postulates once the finite Hamiltonian and observable mode
are fixed.  The Pl\"ucker construction now closes the corresponding selected
rest-mode chain without an independently assigned mass parameter.  For
$z=\psi\wedge\phi\neq0$, the same $z$ that fixes
$\det P_{\rm tot}=|z|^2$ gives an explicit positive-energy eigenvector of
$B_z$ with eigenvalue $|z|$.  Its Euclidean transfer factor is exactly
$e^{-a|z|}$, lies strictly between zero and one for $a>0$, reconstructs the
energy $|z|$, and carries positive visible weight in the scalar transfer
denominator.  These statements are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.

The selection is essential rather than cosmetic.  The other rest eigenvector
has energy $-|z|$ and grows by $e^{a|z|}$ under the same Euclidean step.  Thus
the full $B_z$ is Hermitian but indefinite; the theorem derives the decaying
positive-energy branch, not positivity of the unprojected rest operator.  It
does not derive an interacting action, an infinite-volume Hamiltonian, or an
LSZ particle pole.  The ``positive coefficient'' is the weight recovered after
multiplying by the transfer denominator; the complex-analytic residue in a
local pole coordinate differs by sign and scale.

For the selected factor $\lambda=e^{-a|z|}$, the finite reflected two-time
kernel now has the exact Hankel form $K_{ij}=\lambda^{i+j}$.  Its quadratic
form is one square and is therefore positive semidefinite.  The same theorem
retains an explicit nonzero null vector at two time samples: this one-mode
kernel is rank one, not a hidden strictly positive interacting theory.  An
exact $3$--$4$--$5$ wedge gives $|z|=5$ and $\lambda=e^{-5}$ at unit spacing.
These statements are \NewResult{} \Kernel{} and remain finite two-point
consequences.

The finite many-fermion layer also now contains a genuine locality-and-sector
control beyond free second quantization. On five ordered occupation modes, an
exact even quartic word transfers the occupied pair $01$ to $23$ with coefficient
$z$ and the reverse pair with $\bar z$. A closed unit-phase pair update has an
explicit adjoint inverse, fixes vacuum and every one-particle state, preserves
the full two-particle sector, and is localized algebraically to the declared
four-mode cell: conjugation fixes the outside creation and annihilation
generators and maps the cell CAR algebra to itself. The update nevertheless
mixes the one-dimensional sector containing only pair $01$, so the sector test
is not a disguised identity. These statements are \NewResult{} \Kernel{}.
They do not yet identify the update as the exponential of the displayed
Hamiltonian, compose it with the live HNU regulator, select the lower HNU bands,
or provide an interacting cone or continuum limit.

The formal development also separates ordinary matrix positivity from finite
Osterwalder--Schrader reflection positivity.  Under an explicit compatibility
condition, reflection positivity makes the reflected positive-time block
positive semidefinite and its null-space quotient a genuine real inner-product
space.  Neither notion implies the other: exact symmetric $2\times2$ witnesses
show both failures of implication.  This is \NewResult{} \Kernel{}, but it is
the algebraic finite OS step only.  Reflection positivity of an interacting
lattice gauge-field algebra and reconstruction of its transfer Hamiltonian
remain open.

The result does support a precise statement: a timelike rest gap can be encoded
entirely by massless rank-one momentum primitives, and the gap is exactly the
area opened between their spinor directions.  In the derived walk, microscopic
translation occurs along null channels while subluminal drift and a rest gap
arise from coherent direction changes.  This is an exact property of the
finite model, not yet a universal ontology of nature.

\section{Interpretation and open problems}

The larger null-information program studies a four-term finite carrier square,
positive cohomology, closure interactions, and discrete frame data.  Those
structures motivated the present theorem but are not required to prove it.
They belong in a companion paper unless they acquire a theorem that depends on
$B_z$.

Three claim-discipline boundaries govern everything in this paper and its
companions.  First, every invariant here is a finite-dimensional algebraic
discriminator on an explicit register; we claim no Fredholm or
finite-volume topological index, and any comparison with established
real-space walk indices is made through their closed-form evaluation on
infinite periodic extensions, never as a finite-volume theorem.  Second,
the pair interaction is supplied as an even Hermitian four-mode kick
consistent with the CAR support and layer schedule; we prove its exact
gate, unitarity, and composition behavior, but we do not derive the
interaction from a microscopic principle, and the same gap is open in the
interacting-automaton literature we compare against.  Third, every result
in this paper is machine-checked in Lean~4 with its axiom footprint
pinned; where companion analyses use exact computer-algebra computations
that are not yet formalized, they are tagged as such there and are never
cited here as theorems.

Three frontiers determine whether the construction grows into a physical
theory.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Changing-lattice position-space convergence.}  Compose the
  proved symbol estimates, representative-safe cell averaging, ultraviolet
  tail control, and Fourier identifications into a strong $L^2$ convergence
  theorem for the Dirac PDE propagator.
  \item \textbf{An alias-free exactly local $3+1$ unitary regulator.}  Escape
  the proved successive-axis and degree-one onsite obstructions while retaining
  exact unitarity, finite propagation, the Dirac tangent, and the
  Pl\"ucker-derived complex mass operator.
  \item \textbf{A derived interacting many-fermion dynamics.}  Integrate the
  exact CAR lift and local pair gates into one field-valued action or update,
  then obtain a scattering phase, bound state, or selection rule not freely
  fitted as an input.
\end{enumerate}

The optional research supplement records the finer theorem and construction
tasks behind these three gates.

\section{Conclusion}

The central operator theorem is simple.  The total momentum
$P_{\rm tot}$ determines the radius
$|z|=\sqrt{\det P_{\rm tot}}$ of an odd-Hermitian mass orbit, while an oriented
null factorization selects the representative
$B_z=(\operatorname{Re}z)\sigma_x-(\operatorname{Im}z)\sigma_y$.  This
assignment is uniquely fixed within the stated homogeneous and chiral-covariant
class.  Its positive rest energy is $|z|$, its rest-frame band gap is $2|z|$,
and both close exactly when the primitive spinors become collinear.

The orientation survives dynamics in a precise way.  $B_z$ exponentiates to an
exact unitary turn, opposite histories carry $z$ and $\bar z$, and the walk has
a uniform $O(1/n)$ continuum-symbol estimate.  A common phase is removable,
but transport between local chiral frames leaves the invariant mismatch
$|z_R-(\overline w)^2z_L|$, which splits the exact two-site free spectrum.
An assigned complex mass can reproduce that phase physics; the contribution of
the Pl\"ucker construction is to derive the complex field, its transport law,
its determinant relation, and its zero locus from one null-spinor input.

The same exact treatment identifies the regulator boundary.  The tested
successive-axis $3+1$ extension retains classified zero- and
$\pi$-quasienergy aliases, and no momentum-independent onsite coin removes
them.  The decisive frontiers are therefore an explicit changing-lattice
position-space Dirac limit, an alias-free exactly local $3+1$ unitary
regulator, and a derived interacting many-fermion dynamics.  These are
separate mathematical tests of whether the finite mass-orbit construction can
become a physical theory.
