\section*{Nothing moves slower than the speed of light}

Here is the opinion this manuscript will defend: nature's primitive act is not
an object persisting from one moment to the next.  It is a causal transmission.
That transmission is always lightlike.  Massive particles, atoms, and bodies
are not slower primitive things.  They are stable patterns made from many such
transmissions, and the pattern can drift more slowly than light even though no
primitive link does.\footnote{This is the proposed ontology, not an experimental
fact.  The exact finite model permits only lightlike one-step support and proves
that coherent direction mixing can produce timelike drift.  Standard
relativistic field equations likewise distinguish a null wavefront cone from
the subluminal group velocity of a massive packet.  What remains conjectural is
that nature has this finite null support at its fundamental scale.}

So the deliberately provocative slogan is not that an electron is a tiny bead
racing along a hidden sawtooth path.  It is stronger and less classical:

\widebox{plum}{mist}{\Olabel\quad There is no massive object at the bottom
of the description.  There are only lightlike quantum processes whose
stable, self-consistent pattern is what we call a massive object.}

You already believe more of this than you might think.  \Tlabel\quad About 99
percent of the mass of every proton and neutron --- and therefore most of your
own weight --- is the energy of nearly massless quarks and exactly massless
gluons confined in a small volume.  This is standard quantum chromodynamics,
memorably summarized by Frank Wilczek as ``mass without
mass''~\cite{WilczekLightness,WilczekMWM}.  In the same spirit, a mirrored box
filled with light weighs more than the same box empty: two flashes of light
moving in opposite directions have no individual mass, yet together they have
a rest frame and a positive invariant mass.

This manuscript pushes the pattern all the way down: \emph{every} elementary
process is lightlike, and every mass is the cost of organizing lightlike
possibilities into one persistent, decodable object.\footnote{``Cost'' is not
yet a universal thermodynamic quantity.  In the checked core it is a positive
area, an energy gap, or a turning weight.  The stronger identification with
an irreducible information or action cost is an ontology-level synthesis.}
The sections below show how much of that sentence is theorem, why the complex
phase matters as much as the magnitude, and exactly how the proposal can die.

\section*{In brief}

The proposal can be stated in four sentences.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Events come before objects.}  A fundamental history is made of
        causal transmissions, not little substances carried through a
        pre-existing space.
  \item \textbf{Null comes before timelike.}  Every primitive transmission is
        lightlike; subluminal motion belongs to a composite pattern.
  \item \textbf{Relation comes before mass.}  Mass measures a failure of null
        directions, phases, and local frames to collapse to one simple ray.
  \item \textbf{Decoding comes before particle.}  A particle is a stable answer
        returned by the physical rules: a pattern that can be prepared,
        transported, and detected repeatedly.
\end{enumerate}

We develop that idea on a finite causal grid.  Four recurring structures appear
when the basic transport rule is applied twice and compared with itself:
\emph{aperture}, the opening
between directions; \emph{closure}, the mismatch accumulated around a loop;
\emph{turn}, the conversion between chiral propagation channels; and
\emph{soldering}, the map that ties internal transport to spacetime geometry.
Several finite identities behind this picture have been checked by the Lean
proof assistant, a computer program that verifies every logical step of a proof
(Section~\ref{sec:machine}).  The bridge from these finite models to the full
Standard Model and continuum spacetime is unfinished, and deliberately built
to be falsifiable: Section~\ref{sec:kill} states the kill conditions in
advance.

\section{The picture in one page}
\label{sec:onepage}

Imagine a clock that advances in indivisible ticks.  At each tick, an elementary
amplitude may propagate only along one of a finite set of null links.  No link
is timelike.  Nothing elementary waits in place.\footnote{This is a statement
about the support of the model's one-step propagator.  In units with $c=1$, each
allowed displacement $\ell=(\Delta t,\Delta\mathbf{x})$ satisfies
$\ell^2=\Delta t^2-|\Delta\mathbf{x}|^2=0$.  It is not yet a claim that physical
spacetime has a smallest experimentally observable tick.}

Now allow the amplitude to use more than one null direction
(Figure~\ref{fig:nulldrift}).  A sequence can
zig right, zag left, change an internal phase, or be transported around a loop.
The individual links remain null, but their combined momentum need not be.  The
net object can possess a rest frame and a positive invariant
mass.\footnote{The rigorous finite statement is made with the positive Hermitian
momentum matrix $P=\sum_i\psi_i\psi_i^\dagger$.  Each summand has determinant
zero, while $\det P$ can be positive.  The language of a single object following
the drawn zigzag is interpretive; quantum amplitudes are summed over histories.}

\section{The ontology: events before objects}
\label{sec:ontology}

The grid in the figures is not meant to be a box containing tiny objects.  It
is a ledger of possible causal events.  A link says that one event can
contribute amplitude to another.  A history is a compatible chain of such
contributions.  A physical state is a coherent sum over histories.  Only after
that sum has been constrained, compared, and decoded do familiar objects
appear.

This reverses the usual order of explanation:

In this language, asking whether the primitive particles are massless is
slightly misguided.  The proposal is that \emph{particles are not primitive}.
Primitive reality consists of finite null-information transfers.  A particle
is a repeatable codeword of that process: a pattern that survives transport,
constraint reduction, coarse-graining, and measurement.\footnote{``Codeword''
is used structurally, not as evidence that the universe is a digital computer
running someone else's software.  The exact finite ingredients are a state
space, local update, constraints, positive sector, and observable response.
Calling their stable sector a code emphasizes redundancy and recoverability;
the existence and uniqueness of a full physical decoder remain open.}

This is why the framework is finite without being crudely pixelated.  Finiteness
means that every physical question is answered by a finite composition of
distinguishable alternatives.  It does not require the coordinate grid drawn
on the page to remain visible in the continuum, any more than atoms make a
glass of water look like a crystal lattice.

\widebox{plum}{mist}{\Olabel\quad Reality is not fundamentally a collection of
objects located in spacetime.  It is a finite causal process that constructs
both stable objects and their spacetime relations from the same null data.}

\section{Everything moves at the speed of light}
\label{sec:speed}

We mean the slogan literally --- provided you notice that ``speed'' hides
three different questions.  Distinguishing them is what lets the slogan
survive contact with physics.

\subsection*{Primitive speed}

Each allowed microscopic link lies on the light cone.  In units where
$c=1$, a step has the form
\[
  (\Delta t,\Delta\mathbf{x})=(1,\mathbf{n}),
  \qquad |\mathbf{n}|=1,
\]
so its interval is $\Delta t^2-|\Delta\mathbf{x}|^2=0$.

\subsection*{Front speed}

\Tlabel\quad In a relativistic wave equation, the sharp leading edge of a
disturbance is governed by the part of the equation that actually carries
information from place to place.  If that propagation rule has the form
\[
  \omega^2-|\mathbf{k}|^2,
\]
the wavefront lies on the light cone.  Mass and other local internal couplings
do not change this fastest front.  They do change
dispersion and interference behind the front.\footnote{For an operator whose
symbol is $q(\omega,\mathbf{k})I+L$, with $L$ independent of momentum, the
characteristic set is controlled at principal order by
$q=\omega^2-|\mathbf{k}|^2$.  The conclusion would require re-audit if a proposed
channel supplied additional derivatives of the same or higher order.}

\subsection*{Group drift}

\Tlabel\quad A massive wave packet obeys
\[
  E^2=|\mathbf{p}|^2+m^2,
  \qquad |\mathbf{v}_{\rm group}|=\frac{|\mathbf{p}|}{E}<1
  \quad(m\ne0).
\]
There is no contradiction.  The support of the microscopic propagation and
the drift of a massive packet answer different questions.

\claimbox{The strongest honest slogan.}{\Mlabel\quad All primitive propagation
in the model is null; massive motion is an emergent timelike drift produced by
coherent mixing among null channels.}

The slogan does \emph{not} assign every particle a hidden classical velocity
arrow of length $c$.  Quantum motion is not a concealed billiard-ball path, and
different kinds of fields encode motion differently.  The universal claim is
about the lightlike support of causal influence, not a simultaneously
measurable three-velocity for every component.\footnote{For the
Dirac Hamiltonian, $\dot x_i=\alpha_i$ and each $\alpha_i$ has eigenvalues
$\pm1$, but $[\alpha_i,\alpha_j]\ne0$ for $i\ne j$.  Componentwise spectral
support therefore does not define a joint classical velocity vector.  Massive
wave-packet drift instead follows the dispersion relation and has magnitude
$|\mathbf p|/E<1$.}

The finite theorem encodes each null direction by a pair of complex numbers
$\psi_i$.  If $\psi=(a,b)$ and $\phi=(c,d)$, their oriented area is simply
\[
  \psi\wedge\phi=ad-bc.
\]
It vanishes when the two encoded directions are the same and grows as they
open apart.  Mathematicians call it a Pluecker coordinate.  For any finite
collection, the theorem proves
\[
  m^2=\sum_{i<j}|\psi_i\wedge\psi_j|^2.
\]
Every term is a squared area between two directions and is therefore
nonnegative.  The mass vanishes exactly when all occupied directions are
collinear.\footnote{Technically, the positive momentum matrix is
$P=\sum_i\psi_i\psi_i^\dagger$ and the identity is the $2\times2$ Cauchy--Binet formula:
$\det(\Psi\Psi^\dagger)=\sum_{i<j}|\det(\psi_i,\psi_j)|^2$.  Identifying the
determinant with Minkowski norm uses the Pauli-matrix map
$p_\mu\mapsto p_\mu\sigma^\mu$ and a fixed metric/sign convention.}

\widebox{teal}{paleteal}{\Mlabel\quad In the finite model, mass squared is
exactly the total pairwise failure of the occupied null directions to align.}

In this finite setting, mass squared is not merely \emph{modeled} by
directional disagreement; it equals it, term by term, with nothing left over.
That identity is the seed of everything that follows.\footnote{The identity is
kinematic.  Any timelike momentum can be decomposed into null momenta, so the
identity alone does not explain why a particle has one mass rather than
another; selecting a mass requires dynamics, an action, a stable state,
and ultimately a continuum limit.  Those are the later layers of the
program.}

\section{The number hidden inside mass}
\label{sec:phase}

The wedge of two complex spinors is not merely a nonnegative mass:
\[
  z=\psi\wedge\phi=\rho e^{\ii\theta}.
\]
Its radius $\rho=|z|$ supplies the rest gap.  Its angle $\theta$ records an
orientation and relative phase.  The ordinary energy formula forgets that
angle and retains only
\[
  \text{gap}^2=|z|^2.
\]
That is why an ordinary mass measurement sees the radius but not the angle.
The angle is not absent; it is information that the quadratic spectrum has
compressed away.

\Mlabel\quad The finite theory proves that the complex area determines the
entire two-channel turning rule, and that the resulting energy satisfies
\[
  H(k,z)^2=(k^2+|z|^2)I.
\]
No second mass parameter is inserted.  The same oriented area that the null
spinors determine supplies both the channel-changing rule and the familiar
energy gap.\footnote{Technically, the rule is the unique two-component odd
Hermitian rest operator compatible with the chosen velocity grading:
$B_z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&z\\\bar z&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$B_z^2=|z|^2I$.}

At one fixed point, however, we must concede something important.  A standard
Dirac model handed the complex number $z$ by fiat can reproduce the same
turning rule, gap, and energies.  The Pluecker origin is then a derivation of the
parameter, not yet a distinct observable theory.

\widebox{coral}{palecoral}{\textbf{The reparametrization test.}\quad If the
theory retains only a constant $|z|$, ``mass from null area'' is physically
indistinguishable from renaming an assigned Dirac mass.  The origin matters
only through structures that an arbitrary scalar mass does not supply.}

The current finite theory passes part of that harder test:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $z=0$ occurs exactly when the primitive spinor directions become
        collinear, so gap closure has a geometric locus rather than an
        arbitrary tuning condition.
  \item Oppositely oriented turns carry $z$ and $\bar z$ separately.  Exact
        histories remember information that the spectrum erases.
  \item If the phase varies from site to site, changing to the local chiral
        basis produces an exact endpoint link between neighboring sites.  A
        constant phase disappears; a varying phase leaves connection data.
  \item A finite two-particle pair transfer has forward amplitude $z$ and
        reverse amplitude $\bar z$, while every one-particle basis state is
        fixed by the corresponding unitary pair operation.
\end{itemize}

The largest promised consequence is still open.  A phase winding around a zero
of $z$ should behave like a topological defect and may force a localized mode.
What is already proved is the necessary warning: a globally single-valued real
phase on a periodic finite cycle has zero winding.  Nonzero winding requires
patches or genuine link transition data; it cannot be conjured by drawing an
angle that secretly jumps at a branch cut.\footnote{The live finite theorem
constructs an explicit winding-one periodic link field and proves that no
global real vertex phase realizes it.  The missing theorem is the reduction
from patched local Pluecker data to the existing finite index operator and a
stable localized eigenmode.}

\Olabel\quad The phase of mass is therefore a candidate memory of how the
massive object is assembled.  The magnitude says how far the null directions
fail to collapse.  The phase says how that failure is oriented and compared
from place to place.

\section{Time in steps: the checkerboard picture}
\label{sec:checkerboard}

``Time in steps'' does not mean that an invisible cosmic clock clicks outside
the universe.  A tick is one layer of causal updating: one more opportunity for
an event to influence a later event.  Causal time is therefore order before it
is duration.  A physical clock is a stable subsystem whose recurring phase or
state change lets other processes compare durations.\footnote{The finite walk
supplies an external update count.  Deriving relational or proper time from an
internal clock subsystem, and proving agreement with continuum proper time, is
an open reconstruction problem.}

\Olabel\quad Time does not carry the process forward.  The ordered process is
what gives time its physical meaning.

The grid is a laboratory, not a portrait of space.  Everything it asserts

\subsection*{Why $3+1$ dimensions are harder}

In three spatial dimensions, four directions pointing toward the vertices of a
regular tetrahedron form the smallest isotropic set of this kind.  If $c_i$
counts the number of steps in direction $i$, the finite endpoint obeys
\[
  X(c)^2=\frac{8}{3}\sum_{i<j}c_i c_j.
\]
A history using only one direction is null.  Mixing any two occupied directions
creates a positive timelike interval.

\subsection{Aperture: opening a timelike interior}

``Aperture'' is the opening between occupied null rays.  It is not a literal
hole in space.  Closed scissors present a single blade-line; opened scissors
enclose a wedge of area between the blades.  One ray has zero wedge area with
itself.  Two nonparallel rays
span an area, and the squared area is the mass contribution.  Aperture is the
kinematic channel and the cleanest part of the framework.

\subsection{Closure: remembering a loop}

Start at the southwest corner of a square and travel to the northeast corner.
There are two simplest routes: walk east and then north, or walk north and then
east.  Both routes use the same two directions and reach the same place.

Now imagine that every step also carries an internal arrow --- a phase, a
charge direction, or some other piece of quantum orientation.  The eastward
step changes that arrow according to one local comparison rule, and the
northward step changes it according to another.  If the two changes can be
performed in either order with the same result, the two arrival arrows agree.
If order matters, they do not (Figure~\ref{fig:closure}).

That disagreement is \emph{closure}.  Walk outward along one route and return
along the other in reverse: you have made a closed loop in space, yet the
internal arrow fails to close.  The leftover rotation or phase is called the
holonomy.\footnote{If east and north transport are $U_E$ and $U_N$, the two
open paths compare $U_NU_E$ with $U_EU_N$.  Following one path and the inverse
of the other produces the closed-loop holonomy.  Under local gauge changes,
open paths acquire endpoint factors; a closed abelian phase is invariant,
while a nonabelian holonomy is conjugated and only conjugacy-class data such as
its trace is invariant.}

\subsection{Turn: converting one null channel into another}

For a massless Weyl fermion, left- and right-handed propagation decouple.  A
Dirac mass couples them.  In the checkerboard picture, the coupling is visible
as a turn; in the update table, it is conversion between two
null left/right sectors.  The path picture and the rule-table picture are two views of
the same local event.  Turn is therefore not a bend of a classical object.  It
is amplitude transferred between two propagation channels; the mass rule
sets the complex weight of that transfer.

\subsection{Soldering: tying transport to spacetime}

\section{Why these four belong together}

The unifying object is not the word ``mass.''  It is one finite rule whose
self-comparison separates into aperture, closure, turn, and soldering.  The
technical equation belongs in the footnote; the conceptual fact is that the
four effects are recovered from one calculation, rather than pasted together
afterward.\footnote{Schematically,
$D^\#D=Q_A+Q_C+Q_T+E$, with the four summands carrying the labels used here.}

The four terms are distinguished by how transport, commutators, grading, and
frame variation enter the square.  \Mlabel\quad In one explicit rational
carrier, the four
blocks are independent and their weights can be recovered from
the update table.  That makes the displayed decomposition rigid for that
witness.  It does not prove that every reasonable carrier has a unique
four-channel decomposition; an abstract non-uniqueness theorem shows that an
additional physical principle is needed.

\section{When one particle is not enough}

A probability-preserving one-particle walk is not yet a quantum field theory.
Many identical fermions obey strict sign and exclusion rules, and those rules
must remain local when the number of particles can change.  Interactions
require more: the joint evolution must do something that cannot be reconstructed
from independent one-particle motion.

\Mlabel\quad The finite project now proves the complete fermionic sign rules for
any finite ordered set of possible modes.  It also constructs the standard
many-particle extension of a one-particle update: the extension leaves the
empty state empty, reproduces the original update when exactly one mode is
occupied, and preserves both particle number and whether that number is even
or odd.  Full compatibility with adding a particle, probability preservation,
and inherited spatial locality are the remaining gates.\footnote{The technical
objects are the finite CAR algebra and the determinant-minor second
quantization $\Gamma(U)$.}

There is also a deliberately small interaction laboratory.  Among four
possible modes, a pair-transfer rule converts one distinguished occupied pair
into another with amplitude $z$ and converts it back with amplitude $\bar z$.
A corresponding exactly reversible, probability-preserving operation leaves
every one-particle possibility unchanged but changes an explicit two-particle
state.\footnote{The live theorem identifies a
rank-two pair sector and proves Hermiticity, complex linearity, inner-product
preservation, exact reversibility, and the $z$ versus $\bar z$ orientation
entries.  It does not yet prove that the operation is the time exponential of
a local quartic Hamiltonian, classify it as non-quasi-free in the completed
Fock lift, or compute a continuum scattering amplitude.}

This is a modest result with a large conceptual point.  The phase that is
invisible in the one-particle rest spectrum can become visible in a
two-particle process.  If that survives the completed local many-particle extension,
the Pluecker origin will have produced an operational distinction rather than
only a new derivation of the same gap.

\Olabel\quad An interaction is not a force exchanged between already complete
objects.  It is the statement that the two patterns cannot be decoded
independently: the admissible history of the pair contains information absent
from either one-particle history.

\section{What fixes a particular mass?}

The area identity explains the \emph{form} of a mass gap.  It does not choose
the norms or relative angle of the primitive spinors, so it does not explain
why the electron, muon, or any other particle has its observed mass.  This is
not a small missing numerical fit.  It is the distinction between a kinematic
invariant and a dynamical theory.

The simplest positive action does not solve the problem.  If both primitive
spinors are scaled by $t$, the natural quadratic Pluecker action scales as
$t^4$.  \Mlabel\quad For every positive noncollinear configuration, the exact
scale stationarity equation then selects only $t=0$.  The theory has proved its
own no-go: homogeneous positive Pluecker energy collapses instead of choosing a
nonzero mass.

One can add a competing term such as
\[
  V_c(t)=(t^2-c)^2,
\]
and obtain stable nonzero minima at $t=\pm\sqrt c$.  But then the selected
scale is precisely the supplied constant $c$.  Calling that a prediction would
hide the input in the potential.

\widebox{coral}{palecoral}{The present theory explains why a rest term can be
a Pluecker area and why it vanishes at collinearity.  It does not yet explain
the absolute size of that area.}

\Olabel\quad Our preferred possibility is that observed masses are stable
scales of a coupled many-body and coarse-graining problem: attractors or
dimensional transmutation scales of the full decoder, not arbitrary labels
attached to isolated particles.  That idea becomes science only when a stated
action or ensemble has a stable nonzero solution and a fluctuation spectrum.

Coarse-graining already supplies a warning.  Under the explicit temporal block
$U\mapsto U^2$, a pure mass coin stays in the same family after its angle is
doubled.  The full ordered split walk does not: an exact quarter-turn witness
blocks to $-I$ while naive parameter doubling gives $+I$.  The microscopic
family generates additional ordered words and couplings.\footnote{This is an
exact temporal-blocking theorem, not a spatial renormalization-group flow.  A
physical RG paper still needs spatial decimation, effective observables, fixed
points, and relevant/irrelevant directions.}

The lesson is that the ontology should live in a universality class, not in one
favorite lattice formula.  If null information is fundamental, it must remain
recognizable after the details of one tick have been forgotten.

\section{What could prove the picture wrong}
\label{sec:kill}

A useful speculative framework should become easier to kill as it becomes more
specific.  Ours is now specific enough to shoot at.  These are the cleanest
shots.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Only a renamed mass.}  If every observable consequence depends
        only on a freely supplied constant $|z|$, the Pluecker construction is
        an elegant parametrization, not a new physical theory.

  \item \textbf{No phase consequence.}  If patched Pluecker winding produces no
        protected mode and the $z/\bar z$ orientation disappears from every
        operational quantity, the complex phase is basis bookkeeping.

  \item \textbf{No strict $3+1$ successor.}  If every local probability-preserving escape
        from the proved four-channel alias class loses the Dirac tangent,
        positivity, or locality, the proposed microscopic architecture fails.

  \item \textbf{No changing-lattice continuum recovery.}  If explicit sampling
        and interpolation do not converge to the position-space Dirac flow with
        controlled ultraviolet tails, the grid remains a finite toy.

  \item \textbf{No many-body locality.}  If the one-particle walk cannot become
        a local many-fermion update with a physical interaction sector, it is
        not a quantum field theory.

  \item \textbf{No mass selection.}  If every natural action is scale-flat or
        collapses to $z=0$ unless a mass is inserted by hand, the framework does
        not explain observed mass values.

  \item \textbf{No universality.}  If coarse-graining destroys the null-edge
        structure instead of flowing within a controlled enlarged family, the
        ontology depends on regulator details that low-energy physics forgets.

  \item \textbf{Wrong field dictionary.}  If aperture, closure, turn, and
        soldering do not refine to kinetic, gauge, scalar, and geometric
        effects, the physical names must be replaced by neutral block labels.

  \item \textbf{No Higgs or gravitational completion.}  Failure to derive a
        scalar collective mode and a legitimate continuum soldering action
        leaves two major parts of the ontology unsupported.

  \item \textbf{No empirical leverage.}  A theory that can reproduce known
        dispersion only by fitting free parameters, while predicting no defect,
        scattering, scaling, or regulator correction, has not earned a claim
        about nature.
\end{enumerate}

\widebox{coral}{palecoral}{A theorem about a finite calculation can be completely
correct while its proposed physical interpretation is wrong.  The program is
designed so that those two verdicts can be stated independently.}

\section{The research program ahead}

The next conceptual milestones are concrete.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item Classify every wavelength of the current split walk, then construct the
        smallest local $3+1$ design without unwanted copies or prove exactly
        what extra memory, range, or internal channels are unavoidable.
  \item Derive patched phase links from local nonzero $z(x)$, connect them to the
        finite index spine, and prove or refute a stable localized defect mode.
  \item Give an exact rule for translating smooth waves onto finer grids and
        back, then prove that the resulting motion approaches the Dirac
        equation for a clearly stated class of well-behaved waves.
  \item Finish the local many-fermion extension; then compute a scattering
        phase, bound-state energy, or selection rule.
  \item Replace temporal blocking by spatial coarse-graining, identify the
        smallest closed coupling family, and calculate its fixed points.
  \item Construct an action or ensemble with a stable nonzero Pluecker scale,
        or prove a broad enough no-go to show exactly which new dimensional
        input is unavoidable.
  \item Connect closure and soldering to accepted gauge and geometric continuum
        actions, including their conservation laws and positive physical
        sectors.
  \item Produce a held-out observable whose coefficient is not freely fitted.
\end{enumerate}

The goal is not to make the grid increasingly ornate.  It is to discover which
parts of the visual picture survive every change of regulator and become
continuum invariants.  Those survivors, if any, are candidates for physics.

\section{Glossary}

\begin{description}[itemsep=0.15em,leftmargin=1.35em]
  \item[Amplitude.]  The complex number quantum mechanics attaches to each way
  an event can happen.  Amplitudes for indistinguishable alternatives add
  before being squared into probabilities, which is why histories interfere.
  \item[Null / lightlike.]  On the light cone: a step or momentum that covers
  exactly one unit of distance per unit of time (in units where $c=1$), so its
  spacetime interval is zero.
  \item[Timelike.]  Inside the light cone: slower than light.  The average
  motion of every massive object is timelike.
  \item[Invariant mass.]  The mass every observer agrees on, defined by
  $m^2=E^2-|\mathbf p|^2$.  Zero for a single flash of light; positive for a
  system of misaligned flashes.
  \item[Pluecker area.]  The oriented area $z=\psi\wedge\phi$ spanned by two
  complex null directions.  Its magnitude supplies the finite rest gap; its
  phase records orientation information the gap alone cannot see.
  \item[Decoder.]  The total physical rule that removes redundancy, enforces
  constraints and positivity, and turns microscopic alternatives into stable
  observable responses.  A complete decoder for nature is proposed, not yet
  derived.
  \item[Chirality.]  The two independent propagation channels
  (``left-handed'' and ``right-handed'') of a relativistic fermion.  Massless
  fermions keep the channels separate; a mass term couples them.
  \item[Weyl / Dirac fermion.]  A Weyl fermion is a single massless chiral
  channel; a Dirac fermion is two chiral channels coupled by a mass.
  \item[Propagator.]  The table of amplitudes for getting from one place and
  state to another in a given number of ticks.
  \item[Regulator.]  A deliberately simplified setting --- here the finite
  grid --- that makes every quantity exactly computable.  Results are trusted
  as physics only if they survive removing the regulator.
  \item[High-frequency copy.]  An unwanted lattice wave that obeys the same
  low-energy-looking rule as the intended particle.  Technical literature
  often calls such copies fermion doublers or Floquet aliases.
  \item[Universality.]  The survival of the same large-scale physics after
  microscopic details are changed or coarse-grained away.
  \item[Holonomy.]  The net rotation or phase an internal arrow acquires when
  transported around a closed loop.
  \item[Coframe / soldering.]  The map that identifies internal directions
  with actual spacetime directions, and the requirement that this
  identification be consistent from place to place.
  \item[Principal part / front speed.]  The highest-derivative part of a wave
  equation, which alone controls the fastest possible wavefront.
  \item[Group velocity.]  The speed of the peak of a wave packet; the observed
  drift of a massive particle, always below $c$.
  \item[Proof assistant / kernel.]  See the box in Section~\ref{sec:machine}.
\end{description}
