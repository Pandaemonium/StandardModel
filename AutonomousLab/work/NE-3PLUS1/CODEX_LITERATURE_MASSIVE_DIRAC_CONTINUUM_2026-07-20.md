The massive changing-lattice theorem has now been completed using the same
architecture that the literature recommends. Mass is treated as a bounded
onsite factor, and convergence follows from three ingredients:

1. a second-order one-step approximation to the exact Dirac exponential;
2. stability from exact unitarity; and
3. a telescope or Lax-type consistency-stability argument.

Our live HNU stack supplies the massless changing-lattice theorem, the exact
Pluecker onsite coin, exact unitarity, the correct massive infrared generator,
and now an HNU-specific fixed-momentum estimate for the product

```text
massCoin4 z eps * diracHNU (eps * q)
```

against the exact flow generated by `kinetic4 q + mass4 z`. The landed stack
gives one-step `O(epsilon^2)` and many-step `O(1/n)` control,
four-component changing-cell projection and exact-flow estimates, and strong
convergence in both momentum and position `L2`. The next analytic rung is no
longer the free continuum limit; it is the operator-domain identification and

interactions.

## Primary literature

### Arrighi, Forets, and Nesme (2014)

- Paper: *The Dirac equation as a quantum walk: higher dimensions,
  observational convergence*
- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.3524>
- Journal version: J. Phys. A 47 (2014) 465302

This is the closest analytic precedent.  Their `3+1` massive walk is a product
of exact matrix exponentials, including an onsite mass coin
`C_epsilon = exp(-i epsilon m alpha_0)`.  In Fourier space the walk has the form
`prod_mu exp(-i epsilon A_mu)` with `sum_mu A_mu = D`.  They prove:

- first-order consistency using a second-order Taylor remainder;
- stability because every factor is unitary;
- fixed-time convergence on Sobolev data with two additional derivatives;
- an observational discrepancy of order `epsilon^2` after smoothing.

What transfers cleanly: the consistency/stability/telescope architecture and
the treatment of mass as a bounded lower-order onsite factor.

What does not transfer automatically: their split walk is not the HNU
conditioned-shift schedule, and their theorem does not prove convergence of our
exact endpoint.

### D'Ariano, Mosco, Perinotti, and Tosini (2016)

- Paper: *Discrete time Dirac quantum walk in 3+1 dimensions*
- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06442>

They obtain a massive Dirac walk by locally coupling two Weyl walks on a BCC
lattice.  The construction supports the same structural choice made in
`HNUPlueckerMassiveStay`: opposite-chirality Weyl sectors plus a local mass
coupling.  Their low-momentum particle-state approximation and fidelity
analysis are useful comparison targets, but they are not a changing-lattice
full-`L2` theorem.

### Childs, Su, Tran, Wiebe, and Zhu (2021)

- Paper: *A Theory of Trotter Error*
- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854>

This gives general product-formula error machinery.  It is useful as a source
of theorem shapes, but the finite `4 x 4` HNU target should first use the
project's existing elementary matrix-exponential bounds; invoking the full
general theory would add abstraction without removing the HNU-specific work.

### Arrighi and Facchini (2017)

- Paper: *Quantum walking in curved spacetime: `(3+1)` dimensions, and beyond*
- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.00305>

This construction reinforces that grouping, encoding, and local internal
unitaries are legitimate parts of a higher-dimensional walk architecture and
can recover a class containing the massive Dirac equation. It does not replace
our convergence proof: the relevant acceptance criterion remains convergence
of the actual HNU evolution with its exact encoding, not merely recovery of a
formal first-order PDE.

## Recommended theorem ladder

### MC1: exact mass exponential - landed

The closed-form Pluecker coin is the exact matrix
exponential

```text
massCoin4 z eps = exp ((-eps : Complex) • (I • mass4 z)).
```

The identity can be unconditional in `z`, but the zero case must use the
specific theorem `mass4 0 = 0`. A square-zero identity alone would not suffice:
a nonzero square-zero generator has a nontrivial linear exponential. The live
HNU reduction currently retains a harmless `z != 0` hypothesis in this one
lemma; removing it is a cleanup, not a prerequisite for the continuum rate.

### MC2: HNU kinetic one-step rate in the Dirac basis - landed

Lift the landed two-component HNU estimate through block diagonalization and
unitary conjugation:

```text
||diracHNU (eps * q) - exp ((-eps : Complex) • (I • kinetic4 q))||
  <= C_HNU(q) * eps^2.
```

The basis change should not enlarge the operator norm.  This is a composition
theorem, not a new Taylor expansion of all sixteen entries.

### MC3: product-formula bridge - landed

Combine MC1 and MC2 with a finite-dimensional Lie-Trotter estimate:

```text
||massiveHNU z eps (eps * q)
    - exp ((-eps : Complex) • (I • (kinetic4 q + mass4 z)))||
  <= C(q,z) * eps^2.
```

The coefficient must be explicit or bounded on `|q_i| <= K` and `||z|| <= M`.
No commutation between kinetic and mass generators may be assumed.

### MC4: fixed-time many-step convergence - landed at fixed momentum

Use exact unitarity and `unitary_pow_telescope` to derive

```text
||(massiveHNU z (t/n) ((t/n) * q))^n
    - exp ((-t : Complex) • (I • (kinetic4 q + mass4 z)))||
  <= C(K,M,t) / n
```

uniformly on compact momentum boxes and bounded masses.

### MC6: massive Dirac PDE identification - generator brick landed

Under Mathlib's Fourier convention, the exact position-space generator on
Schwartz spinors has differential coefficient

```text
-i/(2*pi) * sum_j alpha_j partial_j + mass4 z.
```

The `2*pi` factor is load-bearing and affects only the derivative term. The
mass term is pointwise multiplication by a constant matrix and transfers
unchanged. An alternative momentum rescaling is possible only if it is applied
once and propagated through every compact-box constant.

`MomentumRescalingConsistency.lean` now makes that warning executable. For a
positive scale `lam`, a linear generator obeys `G(lam q) = lam G(q)`, the
matrix exponential compensates only after the step size is scaled with it,
the one-step remainder profile is evaluated at `lam * ||G(q)||`, and a
coordinate box of radius `K` pulls back to radius `K / lam`. Thus the
Mathlib `2*pi` convention can be handled either explicitly in the differential
operator or by a single global change of momentum units, but not by changing
the PDE coefficient while leaving the convergence domain and constants fixed.

## 2026-07-21 direct-integral and graph-domain refresh

An hourly Neo4j plus primary-source web pass sharpened the analytic theorem
shape. Standard direct-integral theory treats a field of unbounded
self-adjoint operators as measurable when the fibre resolvent
`q |-> (H(q) + i)^(-1)` is measurable. The resulting decomposable operator has
the maximal domain

```text
{f in L2 | q |-> H(q) f(q) is in L2},
```

and is self-adjoint exactly when its fibres are self-adjoint almost
everywhere. Nussbaum's decomposable-operator theorem is summarized and used in
the modern closed-operator literature; Reed--Simon XIII.85 is a standard
reference for the constant-fibre case. A recent accessible source using the
same criterion is the direct-integral setup in *Propagation and dispersion of
waves in composite media with resonant inclusions*, Definition 2.1.4, which
defines measurability by `(A(m)+i)^(-1)` and invokes XIII.85(a) for
self-adjointness. Moszynski's detailed matrix-measure treatment
([arXiv:2212.13953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13953), Zotero `F5KRJPCA`)
supplies a complementary spectral-representation reference. It was deduplicated
into Zotero, synchronized to the null-edge Neo4j collection, embedded, and
ingested as 50 searchable full-text chunks on 2026-07-21.

This changes the proof queue: `HNUMassiveFibreResolvent` has already produced
the canonical measurability witness for the live symbol. No measurable
eigenvector selection is needed. The remaining Lean work is to prove the
inverse multiplier is measurable and uniformly bounded, lift it to `L2`, and
use its two-sided range identity to identify the adjoint/maximal graph domain.
Because Mathlib does not currently expose the required unbounded direct-integral
theorem, the executable route remains the clean-room PhysLean pattern from
`Physlib.QuantumMechanics.DDimensions.Operators.Multiplication`: truncation for
density, pointwise adjoint identities, and domain inclusion. The direct-integral
theorem now serves as the semantic and theorem-shape audit.

## Kill conditions and anti-overclaim checks

- If MC1 fails, do not call `massCoin4` the exact exponential until the phase or
  normalization mismatch is resolved.
- A derivative at zero is not an `O(epsilon^2)` estimate.
- The generic split-walk theorem in `Compact3Plus1DiracRate` is not an HNU
  continuum theorem.
- A compact-momentum result is not a full position-space theorem without the
  changing-cell projection and ultraviolet-tail argument.
- Fixed nonzero `z` must not be silently scaled with the lattice spacing except
  through the time-step argument of the coin.
- The doubled walk contains both chiral sectors.  Any topological claim must
  track their signed charges; continuum convergence alone does not prove
  anomaly cancellation or a single-Weyl ultraviolet completion.

## Late-evening Fock-locality refresh

Neo4j full-text retrieval and a primary-source web pass expose a second,
independent higher-dimensional gate after the one-particle continuum theorem:

- Mlodinow and Brun's initial construction obtains a local free-fermion QCA in
  one dimension but proves that the same common many-particle construction does
  not extend straightforwardly to two or more dimensions
  ([arXiv:2006.08927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08927)).
- Their subsequent three-dimensional construction starts with distinguishable
  particle registers and restricts to the antisymmetric subspace to recover a
  free fermionic field in the long-wavelength limit
  ([arXiv:2011.05597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05597)). This is a constructive
  reference architecture, not a theorem that the current HNU second
  quantization is already local.
- Brun and Mlodinow's recent QED analysis makes positive-sector preservation an
  explicit interaction problem: strict locality can induce unwanted
  negative-energy production, while extending the finite interaction range can
  suppress it in a one-dimensional control
  ([arXiv:2503.05998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05998)).
- Piroli et al. show in the fermionic tensor-network setting that unitarity alone
  does not imply locality preservation and provide extra simplicity conditions
  for fermionic QCA representatives
  ([arXiv:2007.11905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11905)).

The theorem design consequence is sharp: first prove that the free finite HNU
schedule's fermionic lift maps each local CAR algebra into the declared finite
neighborhood. Then add one explicit even local interaction and separately test
whether it preserves the selected/companion split. Neither conclusion follows
from one-particle unitarity or from antisymmetry by itself.
