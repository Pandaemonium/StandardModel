## Strategic verdict

The strongest unifier is not "all mass is one Pluecker area." It is:

> Each admissible mass mechanism produces a positive or symmetry-constrained
> response operator whose nonzero spectral scale obstructs gapless null
> transport. A physical mass claim additionally owes a common reconstruction
> from that response spectrum to a propagating pole/rest energy.

This formulation preserves the genuine differences among mechanisms:

- a fermion mass is a chiral turn/off-diagonal map;
- a gauge-boson mass is vacuum-orbit stiffness, represented by a Gram matrix;
- the Higgs radial mass is a potential Hessian;
- a neutrino mass is a legal Dirac or Majorana map, possibly reduced by a Schur
  complement;
- a composite mass is a transfer/correlation spectral scale;
- inertial/gravitational mass is a response of the same state/action to frame
  or metric variation.

The common structure is a response spectrum and its reconstruction, not a
claim that these operators are identical.

## Why `3+1` and origin of mass are one dependency chain

The mass story currently has strong finite algebra but an incomplete physical
semantics. The HNU program supplies the missing dynamical setting:

1. an exactly local unitary in three spatial dimensions;
2. a massless Weyl tangent and a Pluecker massive perturbation;
3. a full-zone quasienergy spectrum that can test whether the gap is genuine;
4. a changing-lattice limit that can identify the finite spectral scale with
   continuum Dirac propagation.

Conversely, a `3+1` walk without a classified mass perturbation only solves the
massless regulator problem. The two programs should therefore meet at one
capstone question:

> Does the same supplied geometric turn datum open an exact global HNU gap and
> converge to the continuum Dirac mass term, with no unaccounted mirror sector?

The active HNU massive-gap and continuum work are direct inputs to the eventual
gap-to-pole bridge.

Impact: highest bridge value.

The existing changing-cell and fixed-momentum results are not yet a PDE

### 3. Scoped origin-of-mass classification matrix

Impact: highest conceptual-clarity value and cheapest deliverable.

The matrix must distinguish primitive data, supplied parameters, operator
class, symmetry, positivity, observable gap, continuum debt, and no-double-
counting law. Exhaustiveness is meaningful only after the action/operator class
is declared.

The first scope should cover renormalizable Standard Model mass terms, leading
neutrino extensions, finite composite/transfer mass, and a separately graded
inertial/gravitational response.

### 4. Shared-Higgs-data composition and its exact no-go

Impact: best first new theorem.

The repository now has the local ingredients and a convention-locked
composition structure. Its independent audit found the strongest vector-sharing
reading false: the fermion operator has no Higgs-doublet argument, and the
present types do not select a unique map from doublet space to fermion-operator
space. The honest shared datum is the scalar `v`; the bosonic sectors also share
the convention-fixed vector `H0(v)`. The resulting theorem returns:

- fermion chiral turn from `(v / sqrt 2) * Y`, with `Y` irreducibly free;
- gauge-orbit Gram positivity and stabilizer kernel from the same vacuum;
- radial curvature `2 * lambda * v^2`;
- the Goldstone/radial degree split.

Every supplied quantity remains visible. This theorem does not predict `v`,
`Y`, `lambda`, or a gauge coupling. Its value is proving exact scalar sharing
and independent sector controls while exposing the additional cross-space
bridge that any stronger unification would owe.

### 5. Gap-to-pole reconstruction

Impact: deepest origin-of-mass semantic gate.

A finite spectral gap, transfer decay rate, Hessian eigenvalue, and physical
pole mass are not synonyms. The long-term capstone needs a common spectral-
measure statement and a changing-lattice convergence theorem. The cheapest
first rung is finite-dimensional: express a selected self-adjoint gap as the
nearest support point in the spectral measure and as a resolvent singularity
location. Euclidean correlation decay can then be treated as a separate
transfer-matrix branch.

### 6. Pluecker-to-Yukawa classification

Impact: high originality, higher semantic risk.

The correct question is a moduli problem for equivariant, grading-compatible
chiral maps. Do not assume uniqueness. A theorem that classifies the surviving
space and shows where Pluecker data selects a point is stronger and safer than
a forced uniqueness statement.

### 7. Finite nonabelian composite-mass bridge

Impact: essential for the phrase "origin of ordinary matter's mass," but the
hardest short-term gate.

Begin with a source-audited transfer/correlation API and a finite `SU(3)`
observable. A finite gap is useful only if the state sector and correlation
observable are gauge invariant and the continuum debt is explicit.

## Cheapest decisive falsifiers

1. **Higgs shared-data falsifier (realized):** identical bosonic vacuum data can
   coexist with different fermion operators, and distinct Higgs-vector-to-
   fermion bridges disagree. Only scalar sharing is canonical in the current
   architecture.
2. **HNU global falsifier:** find one unaccounted zero/pi or off-corner crossing
   under the claimed mass interval.
3. **Continuum falsifier:** show the live embedded fields do not inhabit the
   Fourier Hilbert space or generator domain used by the proposed PDE theorem.
4. **Response falsifier:** construct two finite models with the same internal
   spectrum but inequivalent values for a fixed external readout. This proves
   that the readout is not fixed by spectral data alone; a physical-mass
   conclusion additionally requires a declared observable reconstruction.
5. **QCD falsifier:** show that the proposed finite gap is gauge-coordinate
   dependent or unrelated to a gauge-invariant correlator.
