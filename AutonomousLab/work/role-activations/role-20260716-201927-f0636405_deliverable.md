## Literature and provenance delta

### 1. Bare sprinklings cannot select a finite frame

**Primary source:** Luca Bombelli, Joe Henson, and Rafael D. Sorkin,
*Discreteness without symmetry breaking: a theorem*, arXiv:gr-qc/0605006,
DOI:10.1142/S0217732309031958. Zotero/Neo4j key: `HG5ZI36W`.

**Full-text verification:** theorem section, stored chunks 4-6. For Minkowski
dimension greater than one, the paper proves that there is no measurable
Lorentz-equivariant map from a Poisson sprinkling to one timelike direction.
The extensions explicitly exclude a finite collection of timelike or spacelike
directions, including a reference frame, and exclude a finite-valency graph
equivariantly associated to a full Minkowski sprinkling. The conclusion notes
that a finite region can contain preferred directions through its boundary.

**Consequence for active work:** the proposed `1+3` selector must be canonical
relative to declared marked data, such as an Alexandrov interval, base event,
endpoints, protected core, or boundary. It cannot be advertised as a finite
frame selected from an unmarked homogeneous sprinkling. The existing
carrier-causal-time functional is compatible with this boundary because its
endpoint contrast uses marked interval data. This is now a load-bearing scope
condition for `GRAV-ORDER-OPERATOR-001`.

### 2. Causal-overlap spatial distance is the strongest direct selector input

**Primary source:** Marian Boguna and Dmitri Krioukov, *Measuring spatial
distances in causal sets via causal overlaps*, arXiv:2401.17376,
DOI:10.1103/PhysRevD.110.024008. New key: `XJ4JDXW2`.

**Full-text verification:** stored chunks 6-12 and 17. The construction defines
spacelike separation through overlap volumes in the common causal past, then
uses number-volume correspondence on Poisson-sprinkled Minkowski causal sets.
The paper derives vanishing relative error under its continuum scaling and
tests dimensions 1+1, 2+1, and 3+1 numerically. The exact dimension appears as a
multiplicative factor, so distance can be estimated only up to a conformal
factor if dimension is not known. Accurate estimates require the reference
proper-time scale to lie above a density-dependent mesoscopic lower bound.

**Important implementation caveat:** the numerical workflow first uses known
density and embedding dimension to prefilter candidate reference events and,
in one plot, inserts continuum proper time. The paper also supplies a second
filter based only on causal-set counts and reports the fully intrinsic proper-
time estimate separately. A null-edge benchmark must use only the intrinsic
path in construction; continuum coordinates may be used only for evaluation.
The paper's curved-spacetime extension is a proposed local consequence when the
curvature radius is larger than the mesoscopic scale, not a proved general
curved convergence theorem.

**Earlier control:** David Rideout and Petros Wallden, *Spacelike distance from
discrete causal order*, arXiv:0810.1768,
DOI:10.1088/0264-9381/26/15/155013. New key: `RQ6WWH5I`.
Full-text chunks 9, 16-18, and 36-38 document why the naive minimizing-pair
distance fails in dimensions above 1+1 and develop a 2-link/nearest-neighbor
alternative. This is the required negative nearest-work comparison.

### 3. A local causal-set d'Alembertian supplies a concrete neighborhood model

**Primary source:** Marian Boguna and Dmitri Krioukov, *Local d'Alembertian for
causal sets*, arXiv:2506.18745. Existing key: `I72KXVQA`.

**Full-text verification:** stored chunks 9, 11, 14, 20, 22-24. The paper defines
local neighborhoods from order-estimated proper times and causal-overlap
spatial distances, proves expected convergence in Poisson-sprinkled Minkowski
spacetimes under displayed mesoscopic scaling relations, and gives numerical
tests in 2+1 Minkowski spacetime up to density `3 x 10^6`. Its local spatial
derivative requires averaging over many events to suppress first-derivative
fluctuations. The numerical code uses continuum coordinates to choose one
geodesic representative and control finite-box boundaries; the mathematical
operator is stated intrinsically.

**Consequence for active work:** this is direct machinery for defining the
spatial neighborhood graph or kernel in a marked `1+3` selector. It does not by
itself select three coordinate modes, prove a 3+1 finite benchmark, or provide
a Lorentzian connection. The paper is a 2025 preprint in current metadata and
should receive independent numerical reproduction before it bears a headline.

### 4. Scalar propagation supports a path-sum Higgs architecture, not a literal path

**Primary sources:**

- Steven Johnston, *Particle propagators on discrete spacetime*,
  arXiv:0806.3083, DOI:10.1088/0264-9381/25/20/202001. New key:
  `3FEVHQJA`.
- Nomaan X, Fay Dowker, and Sumati Surya, *Scalar Field Green Functions on
  Causal Sets*, arXiv:1701.07212, DOI:10.1088/1361-6382/aa6bc7. New key:
  `4IHIDDGX`.

**Full-text verification:** Johnston's stored chunks 140, 151, and 155-157, and
the later paper's chunks 2, 6-7, and 11-15. The massive retarded scalar Green
function is represented as a mass-scattering series built from the massless
one; on the causal set this becomes a sum over chains or paths with hop and
stop weights. Stated continuum agreements are dimension- and background-
dependent. The curved paper proves or proposes extensions in Riemann normal
neighborhoods and selected conformally flat constant-curvature spacetimes.

**Consequence for the Higgs question:** this literature supports treating a
massive Higgs excitation as aggregate causal-set propagation assembled from a
massless causal kernel plus local mass insertions. It does not show that a Higgs
quantum follows one unique null edge, that every hop is a Hasse link in every
dimension, or that the interacting Standard Model Higgs sector is recovered.
The null-edge model's safest ontology remains: Higgs values on vertices,
covariant changes on causal edges, local Yukawa flips, and an emergent massive
propagator after summing histories.

### 5. Riemannian coordinate and overlap controls do not transfer automatically

**Primary source:** Amit Singer and Hau-Tieng Wu, *Spectral Convergence of the
connection Laplacian from random samples*, arXiv:1306.1587. Existing key:
`MDTBBSKK`; 59 full-text chunks.

The paper proves spectral convergence for connection Laplacians over compact
Riemannian manifold data, including local-PCA frame estimates and orthogonal
alignments. It is a good control for overlap numerics. Its positive-definite
metric, compact structure group, and Riemannian sampling hypotheses do not
prove stability for indefinite Lorentzian Procrustes alignment.

**Discovery-only control:** Peter W. Jones, Mauro Maggioni, and Raanan Schul,
*Universal local parametrizations via heat kernels and eigenfunctions of the
Laplacian*, DOI:10.5186/aasfm.2010.3508. Crossref metadata confirms the
identifier and authors. It was not added during this activation because it is a
Riemannian control rather than current load-bearing evidence and no full-text
audit was completed.

### 6. Finite/internal Dirac geometry is precedent for the Higgs split

**Primary source:** Ali H. Chamseddine and Alain Connes, *Noncommutative
Geometry as a Framework for Unification of all Fundamental Interactions
including Gravity. Part I*, arXiv:1004.0464,
DOI:10.1002/prop.201000069. Existing key: `8HGA475I`; full text added during
this activation.

The paper treats spacetime as a continuous four-manifold times a finite
internal geometry and obtains a Higgs doublet in its spectral construction.
This is relevant precedent for keeping spacetime propagation and an internal
finite Dirac/Higgs sector distinct. Its Euclidean product geometry, finite-
algebra assumptions, and spectral-action parameter relations are not a causal-
set or null-edge derivation and must not be imported as one.
