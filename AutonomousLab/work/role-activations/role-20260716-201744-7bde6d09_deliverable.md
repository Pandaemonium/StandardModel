## Nearest-work comparison

### Causal-set geometry

The nearest established program reconstructs conformal geometry from causal
order plus volume/counting information and defines scalar d'Alembertian and
curvature estimators directly from order intervals. Benincasa-Dowker-type
operators and the 2025 local d'Alembertian work provide recognized scalar
benchmarks. The present contribution is different: it extracts a bilinear form
on a finite probe space and proves an exact Lorentzian-inertia witness. Its
weakness relative to that literature is the absence of a generic selector and
continuum theorem.

### Spectral and manifold-learning geometry

Heat-kernel coordinates, Laplacian eigenmaps, vector diffusion maps, and
connection-Laplacian convergence provide mature Riemannian controls for local
coordinates and overlap transport. The null-edge program adds an indefinite
causal pairing and exact finite no-go boundaries. It must not imply that
Riemannian convergence theorems automatically transfer to Lorentzian samples.

### Discrete Lorentzian and Regge geometry

Regge calculus and discrete Cartan methods normally supply simplicial metric,
coframe, or connection data. The potential novelty here is deriving a local
Lorentz sector from causal order and counts. Until the generic selector exists,
the supplied-frame alternatives remain scientifically stronger as complete
geometric constructions.

### Formal methods

numerical signature observation. Formal verification is supporting evidence,
not the physics contribution by itself. The paper should make the mathematical
statement easy to inspect without requiring Lean fluency and provide replay
