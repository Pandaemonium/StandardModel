## Brief 1 -- Undergraduate (curious physics/CS student, one page)

The Null-Edge program asks a sharp question: can you rebuild the working content
of particle physics starting from *finite* "information edges" -- little discrete
links that carry no length of their own -- instead of from a smooth spacetime?
It is NOT a finished theory of nature; it is a stack of small, machine-checked
theorems plus an honestly-mapped list of what still fails. Here is the honest
scoreboard.

1. **Mass looks like an area. [T]** For the finite pair object, a "mass squared"
   equals a sum of squared areas (`det P = Σ|ψ∧ψ|²`), and the rest operator
   squares to that area (`B_z² = |z|²·1`), with a generalized "cube law". The
   *slogan* "mass IS area" is an interpretation, kept separate from the theorem;
   it does **not** predict any particular particle mass.
2. **Null edges don't age. [T]** A finite edge has exactly zero entropy precisely
   when it is null. "No entropy" is the discrete stand-in for "no proper time".
3. **A tiny interacting automaton is exactly solvable. [T+E]** One built-in
   interaction is solved in closed form (with one disclosed computer-evaluated
   step).
4. **Fermion doubling is exactly booked. [T]** The notorious "doubling" of
   lattice fermions is tracked exactly by an 8-node charge census that sums to
   zero -- nothing is swept under the rug.
5. **There is a positional defect law. [M]** A precise finite rule for where a
   defect sits.
6. **The block-level dynamics selects itself. [T]** At the pair-block level, a
   symmetry argument picks out the allowed family (this is the *static* family
   selection -- a different, later result adds the modular/thermal story).
7. **A cosmological-constant fork became a theorem. [M]** The "everpresent-Λ"
   dichotomy is now proved as mathematics; the remaining step -- matching it to a
   *physical* count -- stays physical, not mathematical. **[C-residual]**
8. **SU(3) falls out of the octonions. [M]** The color group appears from the
   octonionic algebra.
9. **Chiral fermions live on the regulator. [T, draft]** Left/right-handed
   fermions sit correctly on the discrete regulator -- in the draft lane.

And two honest failures, kept in view: the continuum limit is only an L2
convergence result (not yet a PDE/Dirac-equation identification), and there is a
finite-boost **no-go** showing a naive Lorentz symmetry cannot be recovered the
easy way.

Takeaway: this is a *mapped frontier*, not a claim that nature is finite edges.
Every "yes" above is a checked theorem; every "not yet" is stated as such.
