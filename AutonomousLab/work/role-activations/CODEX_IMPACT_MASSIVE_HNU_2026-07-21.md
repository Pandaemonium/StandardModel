# Impact strategy: the massive HNU result

## Strongest defensible claim

The project now has a machine-checked changing-lattice, position-space strong
`L2` continuum theorem for the actual massive HNU walk at fixed time and fixed
supplied complex mass, together with an explicit polynomial resource schedule.
This advances the result beyond a fixed-momentum Taylor expansion.

## What the claim does not say

- It does not select the value of the mass.
- It does not derive interactions or the Standard Model.
- It does not by itself remove companion bands or certify the physical sector.
- Its Pluecker reading is conditional on supplying the walk mass from the
  separately proved null-spinor area bridge.

## Highest-impact next theorem

Compose the continuum capstone with a canonical, continuously transported
negative-band selector and a finite topology-stability threshold.  The target
headline is not merely `walk -> Dirac`; it is:

> An explicit local finite null update has a selected massive Dirac sector
> whose position-space dynamics converge with a quantitative schedule and
> whose topological class is stable throughout the approximation.

## Manuscript placement

Lead the technical paper with the changing-lattice theorem and its explicit
schedule.  Put the fixed-momentum core underneath it as a lemma.  Place the
moving-projector no-go beside the open selector gate: it demonstrates that the
remaining omission is mathematically substantive and identifies the proof
technology that will not work.

## Shortest path to community excitement

1. Land the canonical Cayley rest and commutation lemmas.
2. Prove the discrete adiabatic intertwiner estimate with cancellation.
3. Produce one numerical convergence plot generated from the exact theorem's
   schedule, clearly labeled as illustration rather than evidence.
4. Compare assumptions and rates directly with established quantum-walk and
   lattice-Dirac continuum results.
5. Release a small reproduction target that rebuilds the capstone and guard.

## Impact kill test

If the selected-sector theorem requires a nonlocal projector that cannot be
implemented or encoded quasi-locally, the current microscopic ontology is too
strong.  Present the continuum theorem as a rigorous regulator result and move
the ontology to the encoded physical sector.
