# Impact strategy: continuum plus 3+1

Date: 2026-07-13
Role: Codex Impact Strategist
Work item: QCA-3PLUS1-001, with CONT-FOURIER-001 composition

## The paper-worthy spine

The strongest current story is a **local-to-global obstruction theorem
program**, not a declaration that 3+1 has been solved.

At the fine-tick level, a two-site antiperiodic register supplies a genuine
positive result: every branch moves, the exact inner product is preserved, two
ticks decode to physical translation plus a pi phase, and the auxiliary sector
has no `+1` fixed mode. At the full HNU-schedule level, the ordered Pauli
reflections multiply to the central element `-1`, relocating the zero node to
the pi sector. A free `-I` complement removes a spectral fixed mode but has no
local all-moving realization. A gamma-coupled parent gives a real complement
gap, but its low-energy sector contains paired opposite Weyl chiralities and
therefore zero net chirality.

This is a coherent sequence of exact controls:

1. **Local motion is possible.** The held-branch objection is not universal.
2. **Ordering is physical.** A locally successful refinement can fail after
   the complete noncommuting schedule.
3. **A spectral repair is insufficient.** Local realization and causal support
   are additional obligations.
4. **A local gapped parent is insufficient.** Net chirality and the zero/pi
   ledger remain additional obligations.

The continuum program supplies the publication gate. The exact finite update
becomes a candidate regulator for physics only after its low-energy multiplier
is shown to converge, over many steps and then in position-space `L2`, to the
continuum Weyl/Dirac flow with explicit normalization and tail control.

## Headline claim ladder

### Supportable now

> We give a kernel-checked finite obstruction map for an all-moving 3+1 Weyl
> regulator: local antiperiodic dilation removes the held-sector zero mode, but
> the complete HNU ordering carries a central pi holonomy; spectral and
> gamma-gapped repairs respectively fail locality and net-chirality tests.

This is stronger than presenting disconnected constructions because the same
acceptance gates are applied to each route.

### Supportable after the next two theorem wins

Required wins:

1. exact HNU compact-momentum many-step convergence with a vanishing error;
2. true schedule-indexed transport theorem proving whether the central
   holonomy is passive gauge data or a changed physical connection.

Then the claim can become:

> The exact HNU regulator has the correct controlled infrared Weyl dynamics,
> while its all-moving local completions fall into sharply separated holonomy
> classes; changing the central class requires physical connection data, not a
> basis relabeling.

### Supportable only after the position-space theorem

> A stated dense class of lattice wave packets converges in position-space
> `L2` to continuum Weyl evolution under refinement, with the regulator's
> zero/pi and holonomy sectors explicitly accounted for.

Without this theorem, “continuum recovery” should remain a targeted program,
not a headline result.

## Highest-impact architecture

The best surviving route is an **anomaly-balanced parent/boundary
construction with transported local frames**:

- A local all-moving parent carries the extra sector required by finite
  regularization.
- The physical Weyl mode is a boundary or defect response, not an isolated
  finite bulk eigenstate.
- Schedule-indexed frame transport keeps passive basis covariance separate
  from Wilson-loop/holonomy data.
- The zero and pi sectors are counted together; no projection is called
  cancellation.
- The scaling theorem is proved for the selected physical response while the
  parent remains local and probability-preserving.

This route absorbs the current no-go results instead of fighting them. The
paired-chirality gamma result becomes the anomaly-balanced parent control; the
half-space/index modules become the boundary mechanism; the HNU tangent and
many-step bounds become the continuum bridge.

## Single decisive discriminator

The first result likely to change an expert's view is not another exact matrix
identity. It is:

> A local finite parent with a displayed all-moving update and complete
> zero/pi census whose boundary response contains one oriented Weyl cone, plus
> a changing-lattice `L2` convergence theorem to its continuum Weyl flow.

That package would simultaneously answer locality, unitarity, doubling,
chirality, and continuum objections. A phase-sensitive defect or scattering
observable derived from the Pluecker origin would then distinguish the broader
null-edge theory from a reconstruction with renamed mass inputs.

## Current kill conditions

- If the HNU one-step remainder is not uniformly quadratic on compact momentum
  sets, the proposed many-step scaling route fails.
- If true passive schedule-local frame transport can change the central
  holonomy, the current gauge/physical distinction has been formulated
  incorrectly.
- If every local gapped parent has zero net boundary index after the complete
  zero/pi census, the single-Weyl route fails in this architecture.
- If sampling/interpolation plus ultraviolet tails do not yield changing-grid
  `L2` convergence, the finite walk remains a regulator toy.
- If no observable retains Pluecker phase or geometry beyond a fitted gap, the
  broader origin-of-mass interpretation remains a reparametrization.

## Immediate priority order

1. Close the compact-support continuum slope lemma.
2. Land the exact HNU infrared tangent after semantic review.
3. Obtain the uniform one-step and many-step HNU error theorem.
4. Prove the genuinely schedule-indexed transport/holonomy law.
5. Complete the anomaly-balanced parent/boundary composition with full zero/pi

6. Compose these into a changing-lattice position-space convergence theorem.
7. Derive one held-out phase-sensitive boundary, defect, or scattering

The program should resist adding more finite constructions unless they close
one of these dependencies or kill a route cleanly.
