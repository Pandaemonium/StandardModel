# Five-year plan: 2026-2031

## Planning stance

The prioritized mystery portfolio is maintained in `OPEN_MYSTERIES.md`.

The plan is a sequence of hard exams, not a forecast that nature will
cooperate. Each year has a primary scientific outcome, a control outcome, a
publication outcome, and a pivot condition. Quarterly portfolio reviews may
accelerate, merge, or stop work, but cannot waive annual exams.

Terminology: each year's "target outcomes" are aspirational scope, several of
them open research problems; only the year exam is binding pass/fail. Do not
grade a year against the target list.

## Year 1: foundations, falsifiers, and a stable laboratory

### Scientific objective

Turn the current finite theorem collection into a small number of explicit
theory interfaces and close the most dangerous structural debts.

### Target outcomes

- persistent AFPL operations with reproducible state and cross-model review;
- exact kinematic/dynamical API for null edges, rest operators, local walks,
  CAR/Fock dynamics, constraints, and observables;
- changing-lattice strong convergence on a declared function class;
- a precise Lorentz gate separating fixed-regulator no-gos from invariance in
  distribution;
- state/constraint-selected finite dynamics with every remaining input named;
- gauge-covariant overlap/index interface and explicit controls;
- a finite gravity/Lambda requirements map with no continuum inflation;
- actual intertwiners or no-go results for proposed unification bridges;
- two to four focused papers with deterministic artifacts.

### Control outcome

Reproduce the key dispersion, continuum, index, and interacting-spectrum
claims in a conventional quantum-walk/QFT description that does not use the
null-edge ontology. Record which claims are distinctive and which are only new
derivations.

### Year-1 exam

Pass only if the program has one controlled continuum theorem, one nontrivial
many-body theorem, one gauge/index theorem, and a written list of observations
that would distinguish the ontology. If not, narrow the flagship to exact
finite mathematical physics.

## Year 2: reconstructing local QFT

### Scientific objective

Build a genuine quantum-field-theoretic layer from finite dynamics.

### Target outcomes

- local observable nets or an equivalent operational locality structure;
- second quantization with exact unitarity, statistics, and finite-range
  causality for the live spatial walk;
- interacting dynamics derived from an action, Hamiltonian, modular principle,
  or explicit minimal additional axiom;
- scattering, bound-state, or response observables with controlled limits;
- renormalization/coarse-graining with universality classes and relevant
  directions;
- chiral gauge coupling, gauge covariance, gap/index stability, and anomaly
  accounting at a clearly stated finite or continuum scope;
- an explicit theorem connecting finite histories and continuum correlation
  functions for at least one solvable sector.

### Control outcome

Compare against established lattice fermions, quantum cellular automata,
algebraic QFT, constructive QFT, and effective field theory. A null-edge
construction must beat or illuminate a recognized obstruction, not merely
recover a standard model in unfamiliar variables.

### Year-2 exam

Pass only if one interacting continuum observable converges and one chiral
gauge/index statement survives nontrivial gauge fields. Otherwise separate the
QFT program from the ontological program and publish the obstruction.

## Year 3: spacetime, gravity, and quantum consistency

### Scientific objective

Tie the quantum matter layer to dynamical geometry and test whether a gravity
limit exists.

### Target outcomes

- a random-causal or regulator-ensemble framework with a source-verified
  Lorentz-invariance theorem at the claimed level;
- a scale/volume reconstruction satisfying the Malament split;
- dynamical soldering/coframe variables coupled to matter observables;
- conservation or Ward identities for the coupled system;
- a finite-to-continuum theorem recovering a declared semiclassical gravity
  equation, or a no-go showing why the proposed entropy route cannot;
- black-hole, horizon, or causal-diamond benchmark with entropy and first-law
  content clearly separated;
- positivity, stability, and physical-state selection for the coupled theory;
- an explicit account of where quantum fluctuations of geometry live.

### Control outcome

Benchmark against semiclassical GR, Jacobson-style entanglement equilibrium,
causal-set results, lattice gravity, spectral geometry, and effective gravity.

### Year-3 exam

Pass only if the same coupled model supplies both matter evolution and a
nontrivial geometric response with a controlled limit. Finite polynomial
avatars do not pass. Failure triggers a gravity-program redesign.

## Year 4: Standard Model structure, parameters, and cosmology

### Scientific objective

Determine whether the framework explains observed structure rather than only
hosting it.

### Target outcomes

- derivation or explicit minimal assumptions for gauge group, representations,
  chirality, generations, and the global quotient;
- Higgs/scalar sector or a replacement with correct degrees of freedom;
- mass and mixing mechanism with an honest parameter-count comparison;
- QCD/confinement or a sharply scoped interface to it;
- neutrino mass/mixing and baryogenesis consistency tests;
- cosmological dynamics, including a precise Lambda conjugacy and sign;
- dark-matter/dark-energy implications or explicit neutrality;
- a calibrated simulation suite spanning particle, field, and cosmological
  regimes;
- at least three held-out quantitative tests chosen before fitting.

### Control outcome

Compare parameter economy and predictions with the Standard Model plus GR,
effective field theory, and leading alternative frameworks. Algebraic
coincidences count only when they reduce assumptions or force observables.

### Year-4 exam

Pass only if the integrated candidate explains at least one observed feature
not inserted as an axiom and survives a held-out test. Otherwise the Year-5
product is an impossibility frontier or partial theory, not a complete theory.

## Year 5: integration, prediction, and external judgment

### Scientific objective

Freeze the best candidate, run the completeness exam, and expose it to
independent scrutiny.

### Target outcomes

- one versioned axiomatic specification and dependency graph;
- end-to-end derivations from primitives to benchmark observables;
- explicit domain of validity and effective limits;
- a parameter ledger separating derived, imported, fitted, and predicted
  quantities;
- uncertainty propagation and sensitivity analysis;
- at least one distinctive quantitative prediction or exclusion with a
  feasible observational path;
- independent reproduction from a clean archive;
- external expert review across QFT, GR, mathematical physics, phenomenology,
  and formal methods;
- public record of failed predictions and unresolved contradictions;
- a monograph or linked paper series, not a single omnibus claim dump.

### Final exam

The Research Director and external reviewers grade every item in the charter's
completeness exam. The possible verdicts are:

- integrated candidate theory ready for wider testing;
- viable partial theory with named missing sectors;
- mathematically valuable regulator/representation program;
- falsified or obstructed program with a mapped successor frontier.

Only the first verdict may use "complete candidate description" language.
