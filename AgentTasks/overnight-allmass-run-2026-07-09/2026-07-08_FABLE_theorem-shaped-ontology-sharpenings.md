## Sharpening Layer 2 into theorems

**Mass–energy as AM–GM [M-target, near-trivial, do first].** The Pauli decomposition `P = E·1 + p⃗·σ` gives `det P = E² − |p⃗|²` — the mass-shell relation is literally the determinant identity, which you probably already have implicitly. But sitting on top of it is an extremal statement worth surfacing: `det P ≤ (tr P/2)²` with equality iff `P` is scalar. That is **m ≤ E, saturated exactly at rest**, as one line of AM–GM on the Gram — and "rest" is characterized as the configuration where the Bloch vectors of the null directions *balance*: `Σ wᵢn̂ᵢ = 0`. So the rest frame is simultaneously the maximum-mass and maximum-mixedness configuration at fixed weights, which is the `S = log 2` entry of your dictionary upgraded to a variational principle. An afternoon of Lean, and it anchors everything below.

**Mass–coherence duality [M-target].** Since `det ρ = (1 − tr ρ²)/2`, normalized mass² *is* linear entropy of the direction register, so it trades off exactly against interference visibility of the null bundle: a finite Englert–Greenberger–Yasin-type identity `V² + m²/m²_max = 1` in the two-edge case. This makes "mass = which-direction information" an *equation*, not a metaphor, and it's the cleanest possible P-C payload. Kill: the identity fails beyond two edges without a purity-corrected generalization (it probably needs one; that correction is itself informative).

**The Higgs as a Wigner–Araki–Yanase reservoir [C, with an M-target core].** This is, I think, the sharpest new idea available for the turn channel. The corner gate flips chirality and therefore fails to commute with conserved weak isospin. WAY-type theorems say exactly this: a gate not commuting with an additively conserved charge cannot be implemented by any closed-system unitary that conserves the total charge — it requires an ancilla register carrying charge *coherence*, with implementation error bounded below by ~1/(ancilla variance). The no-go half is one line of finite-dimensional algebra (`[U, Q_s + Q_a] = 0` and `U = u ⊗ 1` force `[u, Q_s] = 0`) — an immediate M-target. The quantitative half turns "the corner requires the condensate" from a MEMO reading into a *theorem template*: the Higgs is the WAY-evading reference frame for weak isospin, and `m ∝ φ` is the standard resource scaling of frame-dependent gates. This connects Q_T to a real, finite, quantum-information theorem family rather than to continuum EWSB, which is exactly the register the program is allowed to speak in.

**Confinement as non-decodability, made precise: the Gauss sector is BRST cohomology wearing a code [C].** `Q_G² = 0` with physical space `ker/im` is cohomological, not stabilizer, but the QEC translation still bites: singlet operators are the logical operators; colored operators are gauge operators that don't act on the code space; and **code distance = the minimal disagreement needed to expose color**. The strong-coupling area law — already M — then has a second reading: the energy cost of exposing a colored codeword grows with the region, i.e. *distance grows with separation*. This hands the `confinementpositivity` job a proof strategy it currently lacks: don't attack positivity of colored sectors head-on; bound colored-sector norms using the kernel-checked area law. Kill unchanged from conjecture B.

**Modular selection: derive the D2 generator instead of positing it [C].** Caveat (i) of the dynamics layer — "taking the sector mass form as generator is a posit" — can be attacked with Connes–Rovelli thermal time on a finite space, where everything is computable. The max-entropy state at fixed budget expectation `⟨B⟩` is Gibbs in `B`; its modular flow *is* the `exp(−itB)` flow. So the generator is selected by the state, and the state by your own relative-entropy/DPI layer. Better: fixing the three channel shares *separately* yields a generalized Gibbs ensemble `exp(−(λ_A Q_A + λ_C Q_C + λ_T Q_T))` with channel chemical potentials — the flow is `B`-generated exactly when the channels equilibrate to one temperature. "Do the four channels thermalize to a common β?" is a new, finite, well-posed question that D5 can already host. Kill: the KMS-selected generator on the T2 sector is provably not proportional to `B` under any channel-share constraint.

## Sharpening Layer 1: the geometry is spherical codes and twistors

**Extremal mass configurations are spherical-code theory [M-target family — and squarely your home turf].** For unit spinors, `|ψᵢ∧ψⱼ|² = sin²(θᵢⱼ/2)` = quarter chordal distance on the celestial sphere, so bundle mass is a *pairwise energy functional on S²*. The first-moment structure is trivial (balanced ⇔ rest, above), which means `det P` alone cannot distinguish an antipodal pair from a tetrahedron — but the **partition mass spectrum** `{m²(S) : S ⊆ bundle}`, which mass-monogamy already organizes, does: k-sub-bundle masses see k-th moments of the direction distribution. Conjecture: *sub-bundle mass spectra are maximally uniform exactly on spherical t-designs*, giving a design hierarchy of mass invariants — and since the relevant energies are completely monotone in chordal distance², the Cohn–Kumar universal-optimality machinery applies verbatim. LP bounds on mass budgets given angular constraints. This is a genuinely new candidate paper (call it **P-K**), it's pure §3-layer mathematics with zero physics risk, and it's the one direction where your sphere-packing toolkit imports wholesale.

**The two-twistor prior art you should mine before writing P-B [important literature flag].** The projective layer — CP¹ fibers of null rays, mass as failure to descend to a single ray — *is* discrete twistor theory, and the specific structure "massive particle = several null constituents" was developed in the 1970s twistor particle programme: Penrose, Perjés, and Hughston showed that n-twistor descriptions of massive particles carry **internal symmetry groups** acting on the constituents (SU(2)×U(1) for two twistors, SU(3) attempts for three). That rhymes uncannily with your strand bookkeeping. Either the strand charge algebra of a k-edge carrier reproduces the k-twistor internal symmetry algebra — a checkable statement on the Cl(4) carrier, and a huge legitimacy anchor for P-B — or it doesn't, which kills the identification cleanly. Either way P-B must cite this or a referee will.

**CP as celestial solid angle — a fix and a sharpening for `cpholonomy` [C].** As written, the wedge triple `(ψ₁∧ψ₂)(ψ₂∧ψ₃)(ψ₃∧ψ₁)` is *not* phase-gauge-invariant (each spinor enters bilinearly, so phases don't cancel). The honest invariant is the Bargmann triple `⟨ψ₁|ψ₂⟩⟨ψ₂|ψ₃⟩⟨ψ₃|ψ₁⟩`, whose phase is half the solid angle of the geodesic triangle on the celestial sphere; the quartic version is exactly the shape of the Jarlskog invariant (a unitarity-quadrilateral plaquette phase). So the sharpened conjecture D: **δ_CP is a celestial solid angle subtended by the characteristic directions of the family modules**, with magnitudes (wedges) and phase (area) as the polar decomposition of one holonomy. And if the family index lands via triality-as-monodromy — the natural fusion of P-F with your Q12, since the triality monodromy group is exactly the S₃ your sedenion work keeps surfacing — then the CP phase is the holonomy *of the generation orbit itself*. The `cpholonomy` job should be retargeted at the Bargmann object, not the raw wedge triple, or it will "kill" on a gauge artifact.

## New dynamics content: scattering gets a theorem core

**A finite Levinson theorem [C → M-target, P-J's missing spine].** P-J currently rests on oracle simulations. The theorem that would give it a spine: *the number of bound states of the mass-barrier walk equals the winding of the scattering phase* — Levinson's theorem, which has known discrete/quantum-walk versions in the index-theory-of-walks literature (Cedzich–Grünbaum–Werner line; Richard–Suzuki-type results) to anchor as [import]. This would tie §8's reflection-sectored index directly to *measurable scattering data*, unifying the protection machinery with P-J: bound states as phase winding, protected modes as pinned winding. A finite optical theorem (unitarity of the walk S-matrix ⇒ forward-amplitude sum rule) is the companion M-target. Together they'd make "mass is scatterable geometry" a chapter with theorems instead of plots.
