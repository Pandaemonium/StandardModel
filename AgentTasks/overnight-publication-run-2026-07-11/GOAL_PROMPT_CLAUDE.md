## Default lane

### F0. Paper A publication architecture

Own the title, abstract, introduction, nearest-work section, interpretation,
discussion, and conclusion. The first two pages must answer:

1. What problem is solved?
2. What object is canonical rather than assigned?
3. What theorem is new?
4. What consequence survives beyond `m = |z|` reparametrization?
5. What remains outside the result?

Use one derivation spine:

```text
null spinors -> complex area -> canonical rest operator -> exact unitary walk
-> phase/local/defect or interaction consequence
```

Do not bury the novelty under ontology, formal-methods description, or a long
inventory. Keep the ontology decisive in discussion after the theorem earns it.

### F1. Nearest-work and referee challenge

Own `REFEREE_OBJECTION_REGISTER.md` and the nearest-work comparison. Search and
read the actual papers, using full-text chunks for load-bearing claims. Compare
assumptions and conclusions, not themes. Ask whether the work is:

- a standard Dirac mass in new notation;
- Cauchy-Binet presented as physics;
- a finite toy without continuum control;
- a quantum walk with unresolved aliases;
- a formalization paper without new physics;
- a broad ontology unsupported by an observable.

For each objection, produce a theorem, a scoped concession, or a concrete next
gate. Never answer a novelty objection with rhetoric.
