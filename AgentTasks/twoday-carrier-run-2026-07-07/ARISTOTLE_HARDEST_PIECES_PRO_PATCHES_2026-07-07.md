## Corrected piece 8: finite Reeh-screen gate

For `V = V_S tensor V_E`, `A_S = End(V_S) tensor 1`, and a vector `Omega` of
Schmidt rank `r`:

- `A_S . Omega = V_S tensor W_E`, where `W_E` is the environment-side Schmidt
  support.
- `Omega` is cyclic for `A_S` iff `r = dim V_E`.
- `Omega` is separating for `A_S` iff `r = dim V_S`.
- `Omega` is cyclic and separating iff `r = dim V_S = dim V_E`.

Do not use the compressed phrase "maximal Schmidt rank" unless the theorem
also fixes the dimension inequality that makes it equivalent to the relevant
full-support condition.

## Corrected piece 11: RG-Schur normalization and interpretation

State the Clifford pair product theorem with an explicit coefficient:

```text
{c(a), c(b)} = lambda I, c(a)^2 = 0, c(b)^2 = 0
implies (c(a)c(b))^2 = lambda c(a)c(b).
```

For the proposed 2-by-2 fixture,
`c(l) = [[0,1],[0,0]]` and `c(n) = [[0,0],[1,0]]`, the convention is
`{c(l), c(n)} = I`, equivalently `2<l,n> = 1` in Fable's notation.  The
effective Schur term may carry a nonzero scalar/sign depending on block
orientation and hidden scalar block; nilpotency is unchanged by a nonzero
scalar.

Use "non-nilpotent effective term" unless the file separately proves that the
effective term is literally Clifford multiplication by a non-null vector.

Suggested split:

- `clifford_pair_product_square`,
- `rg_schur_lightcone_nonNilpotent`,
- `rg_schur_parallel_negative_control`.

## Strengthened piece 1: physical-sector Stage A

Stage A should assume:

- a finite complex vector space with a nondegenerate Hermitian/Krein form of
  inertia `(p,q)` and `q <= p`,
- a `q`-dimensional isotropic subspace `Gamma'`,
- `V' := Gamma' orthogonal complement`,
- `N := radical` of the restricted form on `V'`.

Target conclusions:

- `N = Gamma'`,
- the quotient form on `V'/N` is positive definite,
- `dim(V'/N) = p - q`.

If the theorem also says an operator `D` descends, include both:

- `D` is self-adjoint for the relevant form,
- `D Gamma' <= Gamma'`.

Then `D(V') <= V'` follows by adjointness.  Do not rely on the inclusion alone.
